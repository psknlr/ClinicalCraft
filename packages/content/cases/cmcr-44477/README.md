## Learning outcomes

## Clinical summary

The report clinically diagnosed Wolfram syndrome with DKA; the genetic subtype and diabetes-insipidus mechanism remain unproven. The acute metabolic crisis must be recognised first. Dilute polyuria after glucose control needs a new explanation. Visual, auditory and urinary findings support syndromic assessment.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What must be recognised on admission? Options: Diabetic ketoacidosis with severe dehydration and electrolyte disturbance (supported: Hyperglycaemia, ketonuria and metabolic acidosis establish the emergency pattern.) Seven years of thirst means this is still psychogenic polydipsia alone (distractor: This cannot explain ketosis and high-anion-gap acidosis.) Vomiting justifies treatment as simple gastritis (distractor: Gastritis does not explain the metabolic findings.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Diabetes, optic atrophy, hearing and urinary abnormalities support clinical suspicion of a Wolfram spectrum disorder requiring molecular and water-balance reassessment (supported: An incomplete historical vasopressin test does not prove nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, and no WFS1 result can be invented.) Persistent polyuria is entirely uncontrolled glucose (distractor: Glucose had stabilised; dilute urine and other organ findings require further explanation.) Four features prove a particular pathogenic variant (distractor: A clinical pattern cannot specify a genotype.)
- **management:** How should safe subsequent care be planned? Options: After specialist paediatric DKA care, coordinate water balance, vision/hearing, bladder and genetic assessment (supported: DKA care requires clinical monitoring; normal glucose does not end multisystem assessment.) Copy historical routine bicarbonate for all similar children (distractor: Current paediatric DKA protocols do not support routine bicarbonate; indications require assessment.) Restrict water for an unsupervised deprivation test (distractor: Severe dehydration and complex water balance make this dangerous.)
