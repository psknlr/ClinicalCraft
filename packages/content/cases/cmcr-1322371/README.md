## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise easily missed perineal findings after multiple trauma.
2. Use cord tracing to explain the position of both testes.
3. Distinguish gross anatomy from perfusion and reproductive evidence.
4. Discuss safe timely specialist care and long-term follow-up.

## Clinical summary

Cord-tracing ultrasound and exploration support traumatic crossed testicular dislocation, with incomplete evidence on perfusion and fertility. Multiple trauma must not obscure abnormal genital findings. Cord tracing identifies side; mass location alone is insufficient. Morphology, perfusion, sperm vitality and fertility are distinct evidence domains.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which interpretation best accounts for the empty hemiscrotum, perineal mass and cord tracing? Options: Traumatic crossed testicular dislocation, requiring identification and injury assessment of both testes (supported: The right testis crossed into the left hemiscrotum while the left reached the right perineum; side cannot be assigned from mass location alone.) The mass is only a fracture-related haematoma and the scrotal finding needs no explanation (distractor: Cord continuity identifies testicular tissue, and the empty hemiscrotum remains unexplained by a haematoma.) Only one testis moved to its ipsilateral perineum, with the other normally positioned (distractor: Cord tracing documented displacement of both testes.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Surgery confirms crossed positions and septal rupture, but gross colour does not replace perfusion or reproductive assessment (supported: The record confirms anatomy; absent Doppler and functional data preclude declaring complete normal function.) Homogeneous echoes plus normal colour prove intact preoperative perfusion (distractor: The ultrasound omitted blood flow, and later appearance cannot supply unmeasured preoperative data.) Normal appearance on day twenty-five proves all similar injuries can wait weeks (distractor: This single historical timeline cannot establish delay as safe; fibrous adhesions were already present.)
- **management:** What is the safest improvement to the pathway for a similar injury? Options: Arrange timely urology assessment of perfusion and rupture; use timely operative fixation when reduction cannot be achieved, with reproductive follow-up (supported: The current EAU pathway does not support copying a weeks-long wait or self-reduction, and later function may remain uncertain.) Routinely wait until day twenty-five before considering treatment (distractor: The report delay is not a recommendation and does not exclude ischaemia or rupture.) Ask the patient to repeatedly push the testis back, then omit follow-up once appearance recovers (distractor: Reduction requires professional assessment and monitoring; normal appearance does not establish restored fertility.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Initial trauma and urine records: Earlier imaging recorded undisplaced right pubic-body/inferior-ramus and bilateral acetabular fractures with other right-limb fractures. Abdominal ultrasound showed small fluid collections; the initial urinalysis was negative. A negative urinalysis does not explain the perineal mass.
- Consultation ultrasound and cord-tracing record: Tracing the cords placed the right testis in the left hemiscrotum and the left testis in soft tissue anterior-right to the anus, about 3.5×1.7 cm and 0.34 cm beneath skin, with homogeneous echoes and a little surrounding fluid. Testicular blood flow was not described and cannot be assumed normal.
- Subsequent operative anatomy record: Exploration on day twenty-five showed a torn scrotal septum, the right testis in the left hemiscrotum and the left in the right perineum, with fibrous bands already present. Both testes and epididymides were described as normal in shape and colour. The historical delay and gross appearance do not establish a recommended waiting interval or complete functional preservation.

## 中文摘要

**病例概要：** 超声沿精索定位及后续探查支持创伤性睾丸交叉脱位；灌注和生育功能证据不完整。

**学习目标：** 识别多发伤后易漏诊的会阴异常。；利用精索追踪解释两侧睾丸位置。；区分解剖外观与灌注、生殖功能证据。；讨论及时专科处置及长期随访的安全边界。

**关键决策：** 哪一项解释最能同时说明空虚阴囊、会阴肿块及精索追踪结果？ 后续资料能支持哪一项结论？ 复盘同类创伤的诊疗路径，怎样改进最安全？

**复盘要点：** 多发伤不能遮蔽外生殖器的异常体征；精索追踪用于确认侧别，不能仅按肿块位置判断；形态、灌注、精子存活和生育结局是不同证据层面。尚待核对：缺少会诊时生命体征、超声血流、伤前生育基线、精子活率及运动率和长期生育结局。本稿尚未经执业医师审核。
