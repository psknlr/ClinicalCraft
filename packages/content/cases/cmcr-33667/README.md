## Learning outcomes

By the end of the case the learner can:

1. Recognise anterior visual pathway disease from absent fixing, blink to light and pupil reflexes, and link a
   micropenis, heavy drinking with much urine at night and early sleepiness to hypothalamic–pituitary dysfunction.
2. Read an absent septum pellucidum with thin optic nerves and chiasm as septo-optic dysplasia and arrange
   assessment and re-assessment of every pituitary axis, cortisol first.
3. Assess suspected arginine vasopressin deficiency in an infant safely, as the RCH 2022 diabetes insipidus
   guideline sets out: free water, no restriction, paired blood and urine osmolality, and a deprivation test only
   under specialist monitoring.
4. Plan GH treatment for GH deficiency in line with PES 2016 (a strong recommendation to treat, to normalise adult
   height and avoid extreme shortness), clearing the adrenal axis first and re-assessing the adrenal and thyroid axes
   after starting.
5. Set up the BSPED 2023 emergency plan for an infant with confirmed ACTH deficiency (sick-day doses, an emergency
   card, IM hydrocortisone 25 mg under one year) and monitor glucose against the PES 2015 thresholds.
6. Keep and verify a contradictory published laboratory value without editing it, and explain the diagnosis, the
   risks and the uncertainty to the parents using the Calgary–Cambridge structure.

These are `learningOutcomes` in `case.json`; they name the diagnosis, so front-ends show them before play only behind
the closed **Learning outcomes (6) · may reveal the diagnosis** disclosure and list them in every debrief.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

A boy born **post-term** — caesarean section at **43 weeks + 1 day** for a post-term pregnancy without amniotic
fluid, birth weight **4.5 kg** — whose mother had a febrile cold in mid-pregnancy (better after three days of
treatment; the drugs are not recorded) and whose parents are not consanguineous. In his first three months he
**rarely cried, fell asleep after feeds and slept about 22 hours a day**. At **6 months** his parents bring him in
because for **over 20 days both eyes have turned upwards, intermittently but often**; he still has **unsteady head
control, cannot roll or sit unsupported and does not grasp objects of his own accord**; he **likes drinking water,
about 1200 mL a day, and passes a lot of urine at night**. His recorded weight is 8.5 kg (time point not stated).

**Admission examination.** No fixing or following, **no blink to a bright light**; pupils **left 3 mm, right 4 mm,
with absent direct and consensual light reflexes**; **reduced limb tone**, no head control, rolling or sitting; a
response to sound with poor localisation; **penis about 1 cm**, both sides of the scrotum full, no undescended testis.
No vital signs, length or head circumference are recorded.

**Reports.** Fundi: **bilateral optic atrophy**. VEPs: **prolonged latency, reduced amplitude**; auditory evoked
potentials normal. Brain MRI: **absent septum pellucidum, enlarged lateral and third ventricles with squared frontal
horns, thin optic nerves and optic chiasm**. Urine specific gravity **1.002–1.006** on repeated testing; electrolytes
and renal function reported normal (no values); glucose **5.12 mmol/L**; a water deprivation test was **not completed
because of his young age**. GH stimulation: **0.10, 0.05, 0.09 and 0.18 nmol/L**, read by the report as GH
deficiency; thyroid function normal (no values); Gesell **DQ 35**; a later GH value is **printed as 10.06 nmol/L**,
which contradicts the report's interpretation.

**Diagnosis (the report's).** Septo-optic dysplasia with growth hormone deficiency; central diabetes insipidus
(arginine vasopressin deficiency, AVP-D, in the 2022 nomenclature) **not confirmed**.

**The report's course.** GH replacement was **not given** — the report cites his height range at the time and the
family's finances — and monitoring was planned. At the **9-month review**: **DQ 20**, length **69 cm**, weight
**9 kg**, thyroid function normal. No cortisol result, sodium value, osmolality, sex hormones, EEG or pituitary
imaging detail is recorded, and nothing after the 9-month review.

**The safety points the case turns on.** (1) In septo-optic dysplasia the deficiency that kills is ACTH deficiency,
and no cortisol result is recorded at all: screening the adrenal axis early — before any GH or thyroxine, before an
anaesthetic, before an intercurrent illness — is the life-saving act the case scores as critical. (2) With
dilute urine and heavy drinking, the tempting acts are dangerous in an infant: restricting his water at home
(hypernatraemic dehydration), empirical desmopressin (dilutional hyponatraemia and seizures), or a deprivation test on
a general ward. Today's answer is free water, an intake–output chart and paired blood and urine chemistry (RCH 2022).

**Values with units and derived values** (derived values appear only here and in teaching text, never as facts).

* Fluid intake about 1200 mL/day; with the 8.5 kg weight ≈ **141 mL/kg/day** (derived). Whether the 1200 mL is water
  only or includes milk is not recorded, and the weight has no time point — so the figure cannot by itself establish
  polydipsia. The case teaches measuring intake and output (weighed nappies) instead of quoting a threshold.
* Urine specific gravity 1.002–1.006 (repeated); no osmolality recorded.
* Glucose 5.12 mmol/L (≈ 92 mg/dL, × 18.0); time and relation to feeds not recorded. Teaching thresholds (not patient
  facts): PES 2015 evaluates an infant or child below **3.3 mmol/L (60 mg/dL)** and keeps a confirmed disorder above
  **3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL)**.
* GH 0.10, 0.05, 0.09, 0.18 nmol/L; later 10.06 nmol/L. GH is normally reported in µg/L (ng/mL). With a molecular mass
  of about 22 kDa, 1 µg/L ≈ 0.045 nmol/L, so **0.18 nmol/L ≈ 4 µg/L and 10.06 nmol/L ≈ 222 µg/L** — the second figure
  far above any value expected while testing a child for GH deficiency. This arithmetic is shown only to explain why
  the unit and the later value need the original laboratory report; the case prints the values as recorded and
  converts nothing.
* Gesell DQ 35, later 20. DQ = developmental age ÷ chronological age × 100, so DQ 35 at 6 months ≈ a developmental
  age of about 2 months (derived). If the "9-month review" means 9 months of age (1.0.0's English reading), DQ 20 ≈
  under 2 months, i.e. no progress; if it means 9 months later, about 3 months. The case says only that development
  is not keeping pace and that loss of skills is not recorded.
* Pupils 3 mm and 4 mm — a 1 mm difference (derived), with absent direct and consensual reflexes.
* Gestation 43+1 weeks = 302 days (post-term, ≥ 42 weeks).
* Penis about 1 cm at 6 months; measurement method (stretched or not) not recorded. The teaching cites the usual
  definition of micropenis (stretched length more than 2.5 SD below the mean for age) without a reference value.
* Growth: birth weight 4.5 kg; 8.5 kg (time not stated); at the 9-month review 69 cm and 9 kg. No centiles are
  computed: no growth reference is recorded and the review's timing is ambiguous.
* Teaching doses (formulas and fixed emergency doses only, never patient values): BSPED 2023 — IM hydrocortisone
  **25 mg for an infant under one year**, then **2 mg/kg IV (maximum 100 mg) every 4–6 hours**; sick-day oral dosing
