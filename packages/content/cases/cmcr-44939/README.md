## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What requires reopening the differential beyond JIA? Options: Focal bone disease, severe anaemia and thrombocytopenia warrant malignancy and osteomyelitis assessment (supported: Defervescence on steroids is not disease-specific.) Steroid-responsive fever confirms JIA (distractor: Inflammatory and malignant symptoms can be temporarily suppressed.) No radiographic destruction excludes a bone tumour (distractor: MRI and CT already show significant disease.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Biopsy supports Ewing disease, while modern molecular classification and individual lesions need confirmation (supported: PET avidity is not histological proof at every site.) Every PET hotspot is proven metastasis (distractor: Inflammation can also be avid; integrate staging evidence.) An initially negative marrow excludes a solid bone tumour (distractor: A focal solid lesion requires direct tissue assessment.)
- **management:** How can further delay and disruption of definitive care be avoided? Options: Paediatric and bone-tumour teams should plan biopsy, molecular confirmation and staging and review steroid effects (supported: Avoid repeated empirical steroids or unplanned excision.) Increase steroids until fever resolves and stop investigation (distractor: This can further mask important disease.) Young age excludes malignancy and permits observation (distractor: Young age does not exclude malignant bone disease.)
