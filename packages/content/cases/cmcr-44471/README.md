## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise severe liver failure despite low ALT.
2. Integrate clinical Wilson disease clues.
3. Distinguish bridging support and transplantation.
4. Interpret consciousness changes chronologically.

## Clinical summary

The report attributed liver failure to Wilson disease and used plasma exchange and other support as a bridge to transplantation. Low aminotransferases do not exclude severe liver failure. Multisystem copper-related clues support aetiological assessment. Improvement after support is not equivalent to restored hepatic synthesis.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Does the low ALT reduce urgency in this case? Options: No; severe jaundice, coagulopathy and renal injury indicate serious liver failure (supported: Synthetic dysfunction and organ injury convey risk beyond ALT alone.) Low ALT proves mild liver disease (distractor: This cannot explain markedly prolonged PT and severe jaundice.) Treat only isolated renal failure (distractor: Hepatic, coagulation and copper-metabolism clues cannot be ignored.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: The ring, low ceruloplasmin and overall pattern support clinical Wilson-related liver failure without genetic confirmation (supported: Consciousness deterioration increases urgency; ceruloplasmin alone cannot carry the entire diagnosis.) ATP7B molecular diagnosis is complete (distractor: No genetic result was reported.) Temporary alertness excludes hepatic encephalopathy (distractor: Later mental-state changes require chronological interpretation.)
- **management:** How should the current treatment pathway be organised? Options: Engage a paediatric transplant centre early, with critical support and assessment of plasma exchange as a bridge (supported: AASLD guidance emphasises expedited transplant assessment in Wilson-related acute liver failure; temporary improvement does not establish cure.) Wait until every traditional liver-support drug fails before referral (distractor: This may lose the window for management of critical liver failure.) Plasma exchange replaces transplantation in every case (distractor: Support or bridging cannot be generalised as cure from one case.)
