## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should the assessment expand when bleeding recurs after cavity clearance? Options: Reassess necrotic/infectious and myometrial causes of secondary postpartum haemorrhage (supported: Bleeding and uterine abnormalities after repeated evacuation require more than a retained-tissue explanation.) Continue repeated evacuation for presumed retained placenta indefinitely (distractor: Persistent retained tissue is not established, and a fragile uterus risks further injury.) Absence of high fever excludes serious infection (distractor: Infection and necrosis in this case were not accompanied by prominent fever.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Pathology establishes myometrial necrosis; negative cultures do not erase tissue inflammation (supported: Histology and culture answer different questions and are not interchangeable.) All placenta-like material is retained placenta (distractor: Appearance is not villous histology; repeated pathology did not support that assumption.) Normal coagulation excludes life-threatening haemorrhage (distractor: A local source and necrosis can cause severe blood loss despite normal coagulation tests.)
- **management:** What is the safe management principle at this stage? Options: Resuscitation alongside obstetric source control, assessment of uterine fragility and operative risk, and discussion of necessary surgery (supported: Recurrent shock and myometrial necrosis require a team with transfusion and operative capability.) Continue blind curettage despite the pathology (distractor: Necrotic myometrium increases perforation and bleeding risk and requires reassessment.) Wait for all bleeding to stop with oral antibiotics alone (distractor: Life-threatening haemorrhage and necrotic tissue may require immediate control.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Serial uterine ultrasound: Initially an approximately 28 × 18 × 36 mm heterogeneous cavity mass; after repeat evacuation there was no cavity mass, but the uterus remained enlarged and heterogeneous.
- Inflammatory and blood records: WBC rose from 13.04 to 16.12×10⁹/L with neutrophilia; CRP was about 14.52–15.82 mg/L and coagulation was normal at one assessment. Fever was not prominent.
- Third admission record: During another heavy bleed BP was 53/34 mmHg and pulse 110/min, with pallor and cold limbs; a 70 × 62 × 51 mm cervical/vaginal mass was seen.
- Expelled tissue and later operative pathology: Cervical tissue contained extensive necrotic myometrium and inflammatory exudate; later fundal resection contained no villi. Final hysterectomy pathology again showed myometrial necrosis, with suppurative subcutaneous inflammation; drainage cultures were negative.
