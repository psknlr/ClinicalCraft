## Learning outcomes

4. Coordinate blood pressure, renal and emergency care.

## Clinical summary

The report described an IgA-vasculitis-like illness with intussusception, nephritis, hypertension and cerebral haemorrhage. An old normal study does not cover evolving disease. Multisystem complications require separate recognition. Recovery after combined care does not prove one drug’s effect.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which direction is supported by initial rash and bleeding? Options: An IgA-vasculitis-like multisystem pattern with severe blood loss requiring ongoing abdominal/renal assessment (supported: Normal platelets fit palpable purpura but do not reduce bleeding severity.) All purpura is explained by thrombocytopenia (distractor: Platelet count was normal.) A normal old ultrasound guarantees no later intussusception (distractor: Disease evolves and repeated assessment is necessary.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: The new mass and focal signs have evidence of intussusception and cerebral bleeding requiring parallel emergency care (supported: New emergencies cannot be dismissed as ordinary pain or an episode of the original illness.) Seizure cessation excludes intracranial bleeding (distractor: Later focal weakness and MRI establish bleeding.) Earlier normal renal function excludes later nephritis (distractor: Haematuria/proteinuria may emerge without raised creatinine.)
- **management:** How should safety priorities be arranged? Options: Coordinate paediatric surgery, critical neurology and renal teams for acute abdomen, intracranial bleeding and hypertension (supported: Focal signs and raised-pressure risk require imaging assessment rather than routine lumbar puncture.) Perform immediate lumbar puncture without assessing intracranial pressure risk (distractor: Intracranial bleeding and pressure risk make this unsafe.) Give more steroids and wait for the mass to resolve (distractor: Intussusception needs timely surgical/radiological assessment.)
