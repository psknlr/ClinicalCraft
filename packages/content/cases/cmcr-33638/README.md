## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should metabolic and biliary findings be reconciled? Options: Evaluate citrin deficiency and biliary atresia in parallel, including dual disease (supported: Metabolic abnormalities do not exclude structural disease.) No clay-white stools excludes biliary atresia (distractor: Stool colour alone does not negate direct hyperbilirubinaemia and abnormal imaging.) Normal feeding permits waiting for spontaneous resolution (distractor: Infant cholestasis is time-sensitive.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Genetics support NICCD without excluding coexisting obstruction (supported: Persistent jaundice and abnormal ducts still require surgical assessment.) A genetic diagnosis means there can be only one disease (distractor: Dual diagnoses are possible.) Lower ammonia proves biliary patency (distractor: Metabolic improvement and duct patency are different outcomes.)
- **management:** How should incomplete obstruction confirmation be discussed? Options: Prompt hepatobiliary review should explain timing, invasive-test benefits/risks and family concerns (supported: Do not copy the historical delay; explain the unresolved treatable structural issue.) Wait indefinitely for formula after a positive gene test (distractor: This may delay the biliary-atresia assessment window.) Refusal of testing permits ending support and explanation (distractor: Continue shared decisions and safety planning.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Cholestasis and coagulation: Total/direct bilirubin 188.0/110.4 µmol/L, GGT 152 U/L, albumin 23.6 g/L, PT 21.3 s and APTT 79 s.
- Metabolic markers: Ammonia 156 µmol/L, citrulline 354.86 µmol/L with several raised amino acids; AFP 451055 ng/mL.
- Biliary imaging: Ultrasound suggested atresia; MRCP did not show the common hepatic/common bile ducts and showed a very small gallbladder.
