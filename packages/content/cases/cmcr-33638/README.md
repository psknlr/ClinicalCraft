## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia in a jaundiced 2-month-old (direct bilirubin above 17 µmol/L is
   pathological; NASPGHAN/ESPGHAN 2017) and treat it as cholestasis needing same-day evaluation for biliary atresia,
   however pale-yellow rather than white the stools and however well he feeds.
2. Treat a prolonged PT and APTT in a cholestatic infant as vitamin K deficiency until shown otherwise: vitamin K by
   injection at once (1 mg IV; Konakion MM Paediatric SmPC, 2024) and a repeat clotting screen before any biopsy or
   operation.
3. Read raised citrulline and ammonia with an AFP of 451055 ng/mL as pointing to citrin deficiency (confirmed by
   SLC25A13 testing) while explaining why extrahepatic ducts that MRCP cannot show still demand cholangiography — the
   two diseases can coexist.
4. Explain why a compound heterozygous SLC25A13 result and a metabolic response to a lactose-free, MCT-enriched formula
   (Inui et al., 2024) do not explain persistent cholestasis, and arrange laparoscopic or open cholangiography without
   waiting for the gene result (NHC 2025: best within 45 days of life, poor after 90).
5. Avoid the plausible but harmful steps: phototherapy, a urea-cycle regimen that stops milk and runs
   high-concentration glucose, a ward liver biopsy before the clotting is corrected, discharge to wait.
6. Counsel the family with SPIKES and teach-back about cholangiography and a possible Kasai operation and about the
   autosomal recessive inheritance of citrin deficiency (a 1 in 4 chance in each pregnancy).

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

A term boy (vaginal birth, 2.9 kg) became jaundiced on day 3 of life; the jaundice deepened over about two months,
with yellow urine and pale-yellow stools that were never seen clay-white. His alertness and appetite were normal on
mixed (breast and formula) feeding without solids. At about six weeks (one and a half months) he was admitted and
given a week of medicine (not recorded which) without improvement. He smiles. His father is hepatitis B positive; his
mother and older sister are healthy; no inherited disease is known in the family; he has had the hepatitis B vaccine.

On examination his skin and sclerae were jaundiced, he responded well, he weighed 5.2 kg, his abdomen was full, and
his liver and spleen were each 2.5 cm below the costal margin. Total/direct bilirubin 188.0/110.4 µmol/L (direct 59 %
of the total), GGT 152 U/L, albumin 23.6 g/L, PT 21.3 s, APTT 79 s. Ammonia 156 µmol/L, citrulline 354.86 µmol/L with
several amino acids raised, AFP 451055 ng/mL. Ultrasound raised biliary atresia; MRCP showed neither the common hepatic
nor the common bile duct, and a very small gallbladder. SLC25A13: p.E252K (maternal) and c.852_855delTATG (paternal),
compound heterozygous — the report reads it as supporting citrin deficiency. After nutritional treatment the ammonia
improved but the jaundice persisted. On a later readmission laparoscopy showed a cord-like gallbladder and atresia of
the hilar ducts; the biopsy supported extrahepatic biliary atresia with mild fibrosis. The family did not accept a
Kasai operation; the long-term outcome is not recorded. The report's conclusion: neonatal intrahepatic cholestasis
caused by citrin deficiency (NICCD) coexisting with biliary atresia confirmed at operation and on histology.

**What the case teaches against the recorded course.** The case is staged so that the learner meets the same evidence
in the same order as the report, and is scored on today's guidance: parenteral vitamin K for the recorded coagulopathy

records the laparoscopy only at a later readmission, at an age it does not give. The
recorded course is never rewritten: every intervention's response says what the case does and does not record.

**Safety outcome.** `ward-biopsy` (risk high) and `discharge-review` (risk moderate) are `critical: true`: a
percutaneous liver biopsy in a cholestatic infant whose clotting has not been shown to be corrected (PT 21.3 s, APTT
79 s on record, no repeat result) risks life-threatening bleeding, and sending a two-month-old with conjugated jaundice
and unseen bile ducts home for four weeks risks losing the Kasai window (and bleeding without vitamin K) — either
fails the assessment's safety outcome (`safety.passed: false`) whatever the score, and every debrief and export shows
it. `ucd-regimen` (risk moderate) is `critical: false`: high-concentration glucose in citrin deficiency is harmful but
recoverable once stopped; `phototherapy` (risk low) is `critical: false`: useless for conjugated jaundice and a cause
of delay and bronze discolouration, not life-threatening. The vitamin K preset (`doseSpec` 1 mg IV) checks a typed
dose: a tenfold order is refused and fails the safety outcome.
