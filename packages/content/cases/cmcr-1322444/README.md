## Learning outcomes

By the end of the case the learner can:

1. Recognise posterior-circulation ischaemia in a young adult with recurrent dizziness, unsteadiness and headache from
   central signs (one-sided limb incoordination, limb weakness at onset), and request brain MRI with diffusion imaging
   and vertebrobasilar vessel imaging instead of treating it as vestibular or migrainous.
2. Plan the aetiological work-up of an ischaemic stroke in a 19-year-old (AHA/ASA 2021): the nature of the arterial
   disease, a cardiac evaluation (ECG, rhythm monitoring, echocardiography with bubble contrast), infection,
   inflammatory and toxicology tests as indicated, and the causes of a raised homocysteine.
3. Interpret homocysteine of 18.7 and 29 µmol/L as a risk marker rather than a proven cause, and distinguish
   non-specific hyperhomocysteinaemia (B vitamins do not prevent recurrence: AHA/ASA 2021, COR 3: No Benefit) from
   cystathionine β-synthase deficiency, which has a specific treatment.
4. Start single antiplatelet therapy once imaging has excluded haemorrhage, explain why an unruptured aneurysm is no
   reason to withhold it (ESO 2022), and reject anticoagulation, first-line intracranial stenting and long-term dual
   antiplatelet therapy (AHA/ASA 2021).
5. Manage an intracranial vertebral artery aneurysm in a young adult through a neurovascular team — characterise it,
   follow it with imaging, repair it only for growth, change or bleeding — never on an outdated angiogram.
6. Read a repeat angiogram showing basilar trunk occlusion with posterior communicating collaterals and an aneurysm
   that no longer fills as progression rather than cure, and apply acute reperfusion criteria (AHA/ASA 2026) only to a
   new acute deficit.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

**What the report records (via version 1.0.0).** A 19-year-old man had headache, dizziness (眩晕 in the Chinese record),
unsteady walking and left-sided limb weakness that began three months before; more than a month later his walking
became unsteady again, he was treated locally, and no CT or MRI was repeated at that episode. He had no history of
hypertension or hyperlipidaemia and denied hereditary disease and central nervous system infection. He was admitted to
assess his vascular abnormality further; apart from mild unsteadiness, no other prominent symptom was documented. On
examination he was alert, with fluent speech, a normal cranial nerve examination and normal strength, and poorer
coordination on the right.

MRI showed a right cerebellar infarct. The first DSA showed a 5×2 mm aneurysm of the intracranial segment of the left
vertebral artery and stenoses at the origins of the right superior/posterior inferior cerebellar arteries and of the
left posterior cerebral artery. Homocysteine was 18.7 µmol/L at first and 29 µmol/L later; lipids were normal. Repeat
angiography showed the basilar artery trunk occluded, both posterior cerebral arteries supplied through the posterior
communicating arteries, and the previous left vertebral aneurysm no longer opacifying. The report (titled
hyperhomocysteinaemia complicated by intracranial aneurysm and arterial stenosis) gave folate, mecobalamin and vitamins
B1/B6; at the two-month follow-up he had no prominent symptoms and follow-up continued. No antithrombotic treatment,
no treatment of the aneurysm, no genetic testing and no outcome beyond two months are recorded.

**How the case teaches it today.** The central signs make this a posterior-circulation stroke in a young adult; the
angiogram shows a multifocal arteriopathy (aneurysm and stenoses) whose nature — dissection, vasculitis, another
vasculopathy, early atherosclerosis — must be established alongside a cardiac search. The raised homocysteine is a risk
marker whose cause is looked for (B12, folate, renal function, genetics), not the explanation of everything, and B
vitamins are not stroke prevention (AHA/ASA 2021, §5.9). The cornerstone the report does not record is an antiplatelet
drug once haemorrhage is excluded (AHA/ASA 2021; 2024 Chinese national guideline); an unruptured aneurysm is no reason
to withhold it (ESO 2022). The aneurysm is a neurovascular team decision on current imaging. On the repeat angiogram
the aneurysm stops filling on the same study that shows a basilar trunk occlusion: progression, not cure. Whether he
had new symptoms at that point is not recorded, so acute reperfusion is reserved for a new acute deficit inside its
window (AHA/ASA 2026).

B vitamins are neutral rather than harmful: AHA/ASA 2021 classes them as no benefit for preventing recurrence (COR 3:
No Benefit), not as harm, and a deficiency found on testing would be treated on its own merits. What costs marks is
the omission they tempt — no antiplatelet drug in time — which is the critical item. Every act with a harm text costs
something: the two critical acts fail the safety outcome and `rb-avoided-harm`; anticoagulation, early embolisation and
aspirin before the imaging cost `rb-no-unindicated-treatment`.

**Safety outcome.** `alteplase` (IV thrombolysis) and `endovascular-recanalisation` (thrombectomy, angioplasty or
intracranial stenting) are `critical: true`: no acute deficit inside a treatment window is recorded at any point, so
either exposes him to haemorrhage or periprocedural stroke and death without benefit (AHA/ASA 2026; first-line
intracranial stenting is COR 3: Harm in AHA/ASA 2021); giving one fails the assessment's safety outcome
(`safety.passed: false`) whatever the score, and every debrief and export shows it beside the score. Both sit in the
critical `rb-avoided-harm`. `heparin-anticoagulation` and `neurointerventional-embolisation` are high risk but
explicitly `critical: false`: anticoagulation can be stopped and reversed and an embolisation decision would be
re-examined by the neurovascular team, so each is a serious but recoverable misjudgement scored by
`rb-no-unindicated-treatment`. `aspirin` (moderate risk, `critical: false`) is contraindicated only before the brain
imaging has been read.

**学习目标。**
1. 凭中枢体征识别青年人的后循环缺血，申请含弥散加权的头颅MRI和椎-基底动脉血管成像。
2. 按AHA/ASA 2021为19岁缺血性卒中患者制定病因检查（动脉、心脏、感染、炎症、毒物和同型半胱氨酸升高的原因）。
3. 把18.7和29 µmol/L的同型半胱氨酸解读为危险标志；区分非特异性高同型半胱氨酸血症（B族维生素预防复发无效，3类：无获益）与胱硫醚β合成酶缺乏。
4. 排除出血后开始单药抗血小板，说明未破裂动脉瘤不是停用的理由（ESO 2022），拒绝抗凝、首选颅内支架和长期双抗（AHA/ASA 2021）。
5. 由神经血管团队依据当前影像处理颅内椎动脉动脉瘤：明确性质、随访，只在增大、改变或出血时修复。
6. 把基底动脉闭塞伴侧支供血和动脉瘤不显影解读为进展而非痊愈；急性再灌注标准（AHA/ASA 2026）只用于新发急性缺损。

**关键决策。** ① 入院：后循环缺血，先做MRI（含弥散）和椎-基底动脉血管成像（干扰项：周围性前庭病变、前庭性偏头痛、多发性硬化）；
② 同型半胱氨酸：危险标志，查明原因并继续找动脉病变的病因（干扰项：已确诊同型半胱氨酸尿症、它能解释一切、无关紧要）；③ 抗栓：排除
出血后每日阿司匹林单药（干扰项：原报告的维生素方案且不用抗血小板、肝素转华法林、长期双抗加支架）；④ 动脉瘤：神经血管团队评估、
明确性质、随访，增大、改变或出血才修复（干扰项：本次住院即栓塞、开颅夹闭并推迟抗血小板、不随访）；⑤ 再次造影：不是痊愈，立即复查，
有新缺损走急性流程，否则继续抗血小板和随访（干扰项：已痊愈停药停随访、立即溶栓取栓、全量抗凝）。
