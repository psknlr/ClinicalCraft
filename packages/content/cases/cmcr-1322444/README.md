## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise posterior-circulation ischaemia in a young adult.
2. Compare vascular imaging chronologically.
3. Explain association versus causation.
4. Plan a bounded secondary-prevention discussion.

## Clinical summary

A young patient had posterior-circulation infarction, multiple stenoses and an aneurysmal lesion, followed by basilar occlusion and loss of aneurysm filling. Recurrent symptoms have infarct and vascular imaging correlates. Homocysteine elevation alone does not establish causation. Non-opacification requires interpretation alongside other vessels and collateral supply.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which interpretation best fits the early records? Options: Posterior-circulation ischaemia with multiple intracranial vascular abnormalities requiring mechanistic assessment (supported: The cerebellar infarct and angiographic abnormalities provide structural evidence.) Young age makes this functional dizziness alone (distractor: Age cannot negate a documented infarct.) Homocysteine alone establishes the complete cause (distractor: Elevation is associative evidence and does not prove the cause of every vascular lesion.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Loss of aneurysm filling coexists with basilar occlusion and is not vascular cure (supported: Reconfigured supply and occlusion must be interpreted together; proposed mechanisms remain interpretations.) Disappearance of the aneurysm permits ending vascular follow-up (distractor: This ignores the newly documented major arterial occlusion.) A specific inherited metabolic disease is proven (distractor: The report supplied no relevant genetic testing.)
- **management:** What should the present-day educational plan emphasise? Options: Neurovascular review of aetiology and secondary prevention, individualised follow-up and metabolic/nutritional assessment (supported: Vitamin supplementation cannot replace systematic management after stroke and arterial occlusion.) Guarantee that vitamins will prevent recurrent stroke (distractor: The 2021 AHA/ASA guideline does not establish such supplementation as effective recurrent-stroke prevention.) Arrange aneurysm embolisation solely from the old angiogram (distractor: Vascular anatomy has changed and requires current reassessment.)
