## Learning outcomes

1. Take a focused history of recurrent haemoptysis (duration, episodes, volume, earlier work-up) and plan his
   admission: SpO₂ measured at once, a blood count, clotting, group and save, sputum microbiology and contrast CT with
   vascular reconstruction.
2. Explain why absent cyanosis does not establish normal oxygenation, and measure SpO₂ in a child with clubbing and a
   possible right-to-left shunt.
3. Recognise the STAT3 hyper-IgE syndrome pattern — eczema from soon after birth, a suppurative infection needing
   surgery, retained primary teeth, a very high IgE — and tell it from DOCK8 deficiency, Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome and
   chronic granulomatous disease.
4. Faced with a CT showing enhancing vessels within lung nodules, avoid biopsy and refer for angiography of the
   pulmonary and bronchial arteries with embolisation, checking platelets and giving antibiotic cover first.
5. Apply the Woellner diagnostic guidelines (2010) to confirm the diagnosis with STAT3 sequencing and parental testing,
   and explain to the family a dominant variant that most likely arose in him.
6. Agree a discharge plan with the family: anti-staphylococcal prophylaxis, antibiotic cover for dental and invasive
   procedures, a written bleeding plan, genetic counselling and help with costs.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

**What was recorded.** A 12-year-old boy had a year of intermittent haemoptysis: five episodes in the year, each
about 50–100 mL; an earlier admission had not established the cause. His history held a second diagnosis: recurrent
eczema since soon after birth on the trunk, limbs, face and neck; an operation at the age of one for left cervical
suppurative lymphadenitis; and primary teeth extracted three years earlier because they had not shed. His physical and
intellectual development matched his peers', and the family history was unremarkable. On examination there were
scattered areas of depigmentation, facial scars, small patches of hair loss and finger clubbing; his breathing was
steady and no cyanosis of the lips was seen — SpO₂ was not measured.

The admission results: IgE > 3.0×10⁶ IU/L (reference < 1.0×10⁵), the other immunoglobulins normal, eosinophils 0.086
(no unit given) and haemoglobin 111 g/L; ANA, dsDNA and ANCA negative; coagulation, echocardiography and the CD series
(lymphocyte subsets) reported as unremarkable. Contrast CT with vascular reconstruction showed enhancing vessels within
right-lung nodules, partly accompanied by pulmonary arteries/veins — a developmental vascular malformation, possibly a
pulmonary arteriovenous fistula. Genetic testing found a heterozygous STAT3 c.1909G>A (p.V637M) variant, not detected
in his parents, which the report considered pathogenic. The report's conclusion: STAT3-related hyper-IgE syndrome with
a suspected pulmonary vascular malformation of uncertain type.

The course: no operation was performed because of financial difficulty (1.0.0's English text calls the surgery
"deferred"; see the contradictions below); at the six-month telephone follow-up he had not coughed up blood again, and
whether the lesion was a pulmonary or a bronchial arteriovenous fistula had not been resolved. No angiography, no
treatment during the admission and no SpO₂ value is recorded.

**What the case teaches.** One child, two problems, each assessed on its own evidence. The bleeding: a source has to
be found and the child kept safe until it is treated — SpO₂ measured rather than read from his lips, a large-bleed
plan, CT with vascular reconstruction, then angiography of both the pulmonary and the bronchial circulation with
embolisation in the same session; never a needle into a lesion that enhances like a vessel. The immune defect:
recognised from the clinical pattern, supported by the IgE, confirmed by STAT3 sequencing with parental testing, and
managed for life (anti-staphylococcal prophylaxis, skin care, antibiotic cover for procedures, genetic counselling).
That the report recorded no operation "because of financial difficulty" implies that an operation was under
consideration (its type is not recorded); the case teaches that path against the BTS clinical statement on pulmonary
AVMs (2017, adults) and the CIRSE standards of practice on bronchial artery embolisation (2022), and makes the cost of
care part of the plan.

`confirmation` after reading the follow-up), and the biopsy avoided. Decisions are scored with
`decidedFirstTry`, so a wrong first answer costs marks for good.

**Safety outcome.** `lung-biopsy` is `critical: true`: needling a lesion that enhances like a blood vessel risks
massive haemorrhage and, through a possible right-to-left shunt, systemic air embolism — an irreversible,
life-threatening error; giving it fails the run's safety outcome (`safety.passed: false`) whatever the score, and every
debrief and export shows it. `lobectomy` (risk high) and `discharge-no-follow-up` (risk moderate) are explicitly
`critical: false`: recoverable misjudgements — a surgical referral that the multidisciplinary team can redirect to
angiography and embolisation, and a disposition the next clinician can correct; each costs marks in
`rb-avoided-harms` and is listed as a contraindicated act in the debrief.

## Facilitator notes

### Debrief questions (PEARLS)

- **Reactions.** How did it feel to hold two diagnoses at once — a bleeding lung and an immune defect — in one child?
- **Description.** What did you know on admission, what did the admission records add, and what did the genetic test
  settle — and not settle?
- **Analysis.**
  - His lips were not blue. What would his SpO₂ have told you, and why does a haemoglobin of 111 g/L make cyanosis
    even less reliable? (BTS 2017: the hypoxaemia of PAVMs is usually asymptomatic.)
  - Which of his childhood facts — eczema since soon after birth, the neck operation at one, the baby teeth pulled —
    point to STAT3 deficiency rather than DOCK8 deficiency, Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome or chronic granulomatous disease?
  - Why is an IgE in the thousands not a diagnosis by itself (AAAAI/ACAAI 2015), and what makes the diagnosis
    definitive in Woellner's guidelines (2010)?
  - The CT shows enhancing vessels in right-lung nodules. Why not biopsy? What does angiography of both circulations
    answer that the CT cannot — and what does it offer in the same session?
  - The report records no operation because of financial difficulty. What would today's plan cost the family, and who
    helps them with it?
  - His parents tested negative. What does that mean for him, for his future children and for his parents' future
    children (ACMG/AMP 2015: PS2 needs confirmed parentage)?
- **Summary.** One thing you will do the next time a child with clubbing coughs up blood, and one thing you will
  check before you send a "nodule" for biopsy.

## Embodied mode

  children's ward; the admission results, the genetic test and the follow-up are records (`requires: ["records"]`,

  every condition (`{"read": "ct", "phase": "workup"}` and so on).
- **Who speaks.** The boy is 12 and speaks for himself (the presenting complaint and his breathlessness); a parent
  (`companion`, `answersHistory: true`) tells the story of his early childhood. No `nonVerbalWhen` is needed. The parent
  has no `presentation`: the case does not record who accompanied him or the parent's sex (KIT §5).
- **Facility and capabilities.** `general-hospital`; the case uses `records`, `cardiac-monitor` (SpO₂), `lab-hematology`,
  `blood-bank`, `lab-microbiology`, `lab-serology`, `echocardiography`, `fluoroscopy` (catheter angiography), `xray`,
  `ct`, `surgery` and `medications`; `caseCapabilityGaps` is empty and the in-world demonstration replays the reference
  path in zh-CN and en.
- **What the treatment list shows from admission.** The treat tab lists every intervention's name and dose text from
  the start, so none of them names a later result: the biopsy and the resection read "a lung lesion" (not the CT's
  right-lung nodules), and the interventional-radiology request asks for platelets and clotting without quoting the
  clotting record.

## 中文摘要

**病例概要：** 12岁男孩间断咯血1年，一年5次、每次约50–100 mL，之前住院未查明原因。出生后就反复湿疹（躯干、四肢及面颈部），1岁时因左颈化脓性淋巴结炎手术，3年前因乳牙不脱落拔牙；体格和智力发育同龄正常，家族史未见特殊。查体：散在色素脱失、面部瘢痕、小片脱发、杵状指，呼吸平稳，未见口唇青紫，未测SpO₂。IgE >3.0×10⁶ IU/L（参考值<1.0×10⁵），其他免疫球蛋白正常，嗜酸性粒细胞0.086，血红蛋白111 g/L；增强CT及血管重建示右肺结节内强化血管，部分有肺动静脉伴行，提示血管发育畸形、肺动静脉瘘可能；STAT3 c.1909G>A（p.V637M）杂合变异，父母未检出。原报告结论：STAT3相关高IgE综合征合并疑似肺血管畸形，类型未定；因经济困难未手术，半年电话随访未再咯血，肺动静脉瘘与支气管动静脉瘘的区分未完成。本版病例事实来自1.0.0版的提取，尚未对照原文全文核对；尚未经执业医师审核。

**学习目标：** 问清反复咯血的病程、次数、量和既往诊治，入院即测SpO₂并安排血常规、凝血、备血、痰病原学和增强CT加血管重建；理解口唇不紫不等于血氧正常；识别STAT3高IgE综合征的组合并与DOCK8缺陷、Wiskott–Aldrich综合征和慢性肉芽肿病鉴别；面对结节内强化血管不做穿刺，转介入放射科行两套循环的血管造影并同期栓塞；按Woellner诊断指南（2010）用STAT3测序和父母验证确诊；与家人商定包括预防用药、操作前抗生素、书面再咯血预案、遗传咨询和费用帮助的出院计划。

**关键决策：** ① 入院首要安排（测SpO₂、血常规、凝血、备血、痰检、增强CT、床旁吸引和氧气）；② 追查哪种诊断（STAT3高IgE综合征）；③ 肺部病变下一步（肺动脉和支气管动脉造影并同期栓塞，不穿刺、不首选切除）；④ 如何确诊（STAT3测序加父母验证）；⑤ 出院计划（长期免疫随访与预防、安排栓塞、牙科前抗生素、再咯血预案、遗传咨询、费用帮助）。

**复盘要点：** 血氧要测，不能看；肺动静脉畸形是右向左分流，BTS（2017，成人）主张影像可见者都转介栓塞、操作前预防性使用抗生素、考虑HHT；CIRSE（2022）：反复咯血见病理性动脉即栓塞，手术留给破坏性肺病或栓塞失败；IgE极高并不特异，确诊需要STAT3变异（Woellner 2010）；STAT3高IgE综合征需终身以并发症为中心管理（AAAAI/ACAAI 2015；Tsilifis 2021）；父母阴性提示新发变异，他的每个孩子有50%的机会遗传（ACMG/AMP 2015：亲子关系确认后才算PS2）。
