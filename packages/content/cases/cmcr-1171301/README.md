## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How can ocular, neurological and urinary findings be unified? Options: Consider an oculocerebrorenal syndrome such as Lowe and proximal tubular dysfunction (supported: Congenital eye disease and tubular findings provide a fuller explanation than isolated motor delay.) Isolated cerebral motor delay explains aminoaciduria (distractor: Tubular findings require integration.) Hypotonia and absent reflexes prove SMA (distractor: This combination is non-specific and ocular/renal clues remain.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: OCRL1 plus the three-system phenotype supports Lowe; negative maternal blood does not make recurrence risk zero (supported: Genetic counselling should explain residual uncertainty such as mosaicism.) Negative maternal testing proves no family recurrence risk (distractor: Tested tissue and sensitivity have limits.) Proteinuria automatically means glomerular nephrotic syndrome (distractor: Aminoaciduria and low-molecular-weight protein suggest tubular dysfunction.)
- **management:** What ongoing support is appropriate? Options: Renal, eye, developmental and genetic teams should coordinate acid-base/nutritional care, vision/hearing and family support (supported: Genetic diagnosis does not replace functional care.) Genetic confirmation removes renal/ocular follow-up needs (distractor: Organ complications require longitudinal monitoring.) Normal future milestones can be guaranteed (distractor: No long-term outcome supports that guarantee.)
