## Learning outcomes

After the case the learner can:

1. Weigh testicular volume against virilisation — stage III–IV pubic hair and a 9 cm penis with testes of 5–6 mL — to
   keep a peripheral source in view while the axis is tested, and give no puberty-suppressing drug before a diagnosis.
2. Interpret testosterone with a GnRH-stimulated LH peak of 0.1 IU/L (below the assay-specific prepubertal limit of about
   3.3–5.0 IU/L cited by the ESPE/LWPES consensus, 2009) as gonadotropin-independent precocity, and explain why a GnRH
   analogue cannot treat it.
3. Avoid premature closure on 47,XXY: explain that Klinefelter syndrome causes hypogonadism rather than precocity, and
   search for an hCG-secreting germ-cell tumour — β-hCG and AFP, mediastinal imaging first, because the EAA guideline
   (2021) places these tumours mainly in the mediastinum and NCI PDQ (2024) lists Klinefelter syndrome as a risk for them.
4. Before any sedation or general anaesthetic for an anterior mediastinal mass, assess airway and circulatory compression
   and hold a multidisciplinary discussion as the SPA case guide (2024) describes, then plan complete resection.
5. Plan follow-up after resection: β-hCG, AFP and imaging as NCI PDQ (2024) describes; testosterone, LH, growth and bone
   age for secondary central puberty; and Klinefelter care per the EAA guideline (2021) — genetic counselling, learning
   support, and no testosterone before puberty needs it.
6. Examine a child's genitals with an explanation, the parent's consent, the child's agreement and a chaperone agreed
   with them, and explain the results to the boy and his parent in stages and in plain words.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

**Presentation.** A boy of 8 years 4 months, brought by his parent, with six months of penile enlargement (the penis
"getting thicker and longer") and voice deepening; he has acne, a laryngeal prominence and erections, no nocturnal
emissions, and has grown 8 cm and gained 5 kg in six months. His growth and development have been behind since he was
little; his schoolwork is rather poor and he has difficulty communicating with classmates. Born breech at 39 weeks,
2.2 kg, with no history of distress; left indirect inguinal hernia repair at 3 years 2 months. His parents are
non-consanguineous and healthy; his younger brother's growth and genitals show no abnormality. His notes record a
weight of 30 kg (time not recorded); no vital signs, medicines, allergies or vaccinations are recorded.

**Examination.** Height 132 cm; acne on the face, chest and back; axillary hair; pubic hair stage III–IV; penis 9 cm
long, 8 cm in circumference; both testes 5–6 mL. The testes are past prepubertal size (≥ 4 mL) but small for this
degree of virilisation — the case's first clue.

**First results.** Testosterone 55.5 nmol/L; GnRH-stimulated LH peak 0.1 IU/L, FSH peak 2.15 IU/L (basal values,
reference ranges and the stimulating agent are not recorded); bone age 11 years (2 years 8 months ahead of his age);
pituitary MRI and imaging of the adrenals, testes, etc. without abnormality (modality not recorded); two karyotypes
47,XXY. A suppressed LH with this testosterone is **gonadotropin-independent (peripheral) precocious puberty**; the
karyotype is Klinefelter syndrome, which on its own causes hypogonadism, not precocity.

**The source.** Subsequently an anterosuperior mediastinal mass was found, with β-hCG 16.0 IU/L (local upper limit 5.3;
about three times it). An approximately 8×5×4 cm mass was resected; histology: cystic-solid teratoma (grade, other
germ-cell elements, approach and completeness of resection not recorded). The report's diagnosis is Klinefelter
syndrome with a mediastinal teratoma and peripheral precocious puberty.

**Outcome.** At one year of follow-up virilisation had slowed; testosterone 8.49 nmol/L, β-hCG 0.7 IU/L, chest
radiograph without abnormality; because of these results the MRI was not repeated, and the report still urges vigilance
for residual disease. The fall in β-hCG and the slowing of virilisation after resection support hCG from the tumour as
the driver, but with the grade unrecorded and AFP not carried over, the case does not claim more. A testosterone of
8.49 nmol/L at one year is still unexplained in the record (LH, FSH and bone age at follow-up are not recorded): central
puberty secondary to the androgen exposure, or residual tumour, are the questions a facilitator can raise.

### Decisions

1. **`first-line` (admission) — the first tests, before any drug.** Key `testes-vs-virilisation`: weigh the 5–6 mL
   testes against stage III–IV pubic hair and a 9 cm penis; testosterone, basal LH/FSH with a GnRH stimulation test and a
   bone age; if LH does not rise, adrenal androgens, β-hCG, AFP and imaging next; no drug before a diagnosis (ESPE/LWPES
   2009: sensitive LH assays; stimulation read for the agent used; sex steroids support but do not make the diagnosis).
   Distractors: `gnrha-first` (treat as central puberty today), `wait-and-review` (watch three months), `adrenal-only`
   (adrenal tests and trial hydrocortisone).
2. **`axis` (workup) — reading testosterone 55.5 nmol/L with an LH peak of 0.1 IU/L.** Key
   `peripheral-axis-suppressed`: gonadotropin-independent precocity; the LH peak is far below the assay-specific
   prepubertal limit of about 3.3–5.0 IU/L (ESPE/LWPES 2009; cut-offs depend on stimulus, timing and assay — 2019
   consortium update); a GnRH analogue cannot treat it. Distractors: `central-progressive` (badly timed sampling),
   `lab-error` (stop and repeat), `cah-hydrocortisone`.
3. **`source-search` (workup) — 47,XXY with peripheral precocity.** Key `hcg-tumour-search`: Klinefelter syndrome causes
   hypogonadism, not precocity; two enlarged testes with no adrenal or testicular lesion suggest an hCG-like stimulus;
   β-hCG and AFP, chest (mediastinal) imaging first, and brain imaging covering the pineal and suprasellar regions (EAA
   2021; NCI PDQ 2024). Distractors: `ks-explains-discharge` (premature closure), `fmpp-block` (anti-androgen and
   aromatase inhibitor for presumed testotoxicosis), `testis-biopsy`.
4. **`mass-plan` (search) — an anterosuperior mediastinal mass with β-hCG 16.0 IU/L.** Key `assess-then-resect`:
   multidisciplinary team; airway and circulation assessment before any sedation or anaesthetic; plan the anaesthetic
   (spontaneous breathing); complete resection for full histology with baseline markers (SPA 2024; NCI PDQ 2024).
   Distractors: `chemo-on-markers`, `sedated-biopsy-now` (sedation in the CT room without the anaesthetic review),
   `suppress-and-watch`.
5. **`aftercare` (theatre) — after a resected cystic-solid teratoma.** Key `markers-imaging-puberty-ks`: β-hCG and AFP
   (e.g. monthly for 6 months, then 3-monthly to 2 years) with chest imaging (NCI PDQ 2024); testosterone, LH, growth
   and bone age for secondary central puberty; genetic counselling and learning and speech support, with androgens and
   fertility revisited at adolescence (EAA 2021). Distractors: `stop-when-normal`, `testosterone-now`, `gnrha-routine`.

### Critical items

`rb-tumour-search` (the missed life-saving act: refusing to stop at the karyotype and actually ordering the β-hCG/AFP and
chest imaging that find the tumour — the phase that reveals the mass opens only after the decision, so the orders cannot
be credited in hindsight) and `rb-airway-first` (the dangerous error: an anaesthetic or sedation for an anterior
mediastinal mass before the airway and circulation are assessed). Both are acts; neither reads a final-phase record.

**Safety outcome.** `resection` and `sedation` are `critical: true`: an operation under general anaesthesia given before
the airway and circulation assessment, or sedation or an anaesthetic for a scan or procedure given once a mediastinal tumour is being sought (decision 3
answered) and before that assessment, risks airway or circulatory collapse (SPA case guide 2024), so either fails the assessment's safety outcome
(`safety.passed: false`) whatever the score, and every debrief and export shows it. `chemo-now` (`risk: "high"`) is
`critical: false`: starting chemotherapy without histology is a serious misjudgement, but one the tumour board and the
next clinician stop before harm is done; `gnrha` and `discharge-now` (`risk: "moderate"`) are `critical: false` as
recoverable misjudgements. The reference path makes no critical or dose error (no dose presets are needed: the
reference path gives no medicine, no item belongs to the engine's high-alert classes — insulins, potassium, heparins,
opioids, thrombolytics — and the drug traps stand for a class or a protocol without a dose).

## Facilitator notes

### Debrief questions (PEARLS)

- **Reactions.** How did it feel to examine and talk about an 8-year-old's genitals? What did you do to protect him?
- **Description.** In two sentences: what did the testes tell you before any result, and what did the LH result add?
- **Analysis.**
  - Why were testes of 5–6 mL "small" here, and what would they have been in central puberty at pubic hair stage III–IV?
  - What does an LH peak of 0.1 IU/L with a testosterone of 55.5 nmol/L mean for the axis — and for a GnRH analogue?
  - Klinefelter syndrome usually means low testosterone and high LH. Why did the karyotype make a tumour *more* likely
    here (EAA 2021; NCI PDQ 2024)? Where else can hCG-secreting tumours sit (brain, mediastinum, liver, retroperitoneum)?
  - The chest scan shows a mass. What could happen at induction, and what would you need to know first (SPA 2024)?
  - The β-hCG was about three times the local upper limit. Does that prove malignancy? What does "teratoma" leave open
    (grade, other germ-cell elements — none recorded)?
  - At one year the β-hCG was 0.7 IU/L but testosterone 8.49 nmol/L. What two explanations would you test, and how?
  - The report did not repeat the MRI because of the one-year results (slowed virilisation, testosterone 8.49 nmol/L,
    β-hCG 0.7 IU/L, a chest radiograph without abnormality). How does that compare with NCI PDQ's surveillance
    suggestions?
- **Summary.** What will you do next time an early-puberty boy's testes do not match his virilisation?
