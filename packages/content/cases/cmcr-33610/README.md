## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What evaluation follows high testosterone with a low LH response? Options: Peripheral precocious puberty requiring a search for ectopic hormone production (supported: The low LH response does not support isolated central activation.) This must be ordinary early central puberty (distractor: High testosterone with suppressed gonadotropins needs explanation.) 47,XXY ends the causal work-up (distractor: A coexisting syndrome does not localise the cause of precocity.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Chest histology and β-hCG support tumour-associated peripheral precocity (supported: Postoperative trends help, but surveillance remains necessary.) Normal testicular ultrasound excludes every germ-cell tumour (distractor: Tumours may be extragonadal.) - **management:** What continuing care is appropriate after resection? Self-treat puberty based solely on the old report (distractor: Cause and treatment require specialist individualisation.)
