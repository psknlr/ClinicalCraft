## Learning outcomes

1. Avoid diagnostic anchoring on diabetes.
2. Compare rapid and gradual proteinuria.
3. Integrate light, immune and electron microscopy.
4. Discuss coordinated specialist monitoring.

## Clinical summary

The report diagnosed type 1 diabetes with steroid-sensitive minimal-change nephrotic syndrome. Onset speed challenges automatic attribution to existing disease. Tissue requires integrated interpretation. Combined treatment requires metabolic risk management.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should abrupt nephrotic syndrome be attributed? Options: Consider non-diabetic or coexisting renal disease rather than assigning cause from diabetes alone (supported: Short duration and rapid heavy proteinuria require reclassification.) Diabetes necessarily means advanced diabetic nephropathy (distractor: A prior label does not replace chronology and tissue evidence.) The twin’s normal urine excludes kidney disease in this child (distractor: Sibling results cannot negate this patient’s proteinuria.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: The overall tissue and onset pattern support the report’s minimal-change interpretation while retaining basement-membrane changes as context (supported: One thickening feature must not override all other evidence, and mild fusion must not be exaggerated as diffuse fusion.) Extensive immune-complex deposition is proven (distractor: Immunofluorescence and electron microscopy do not support it.) All these changes are necessarily irreversible (distractor: The evidence does not establish that prognosis.)
- **management:** Which coordination is central to treatment teaching? Options: Paediatric renal and endocrine teams should coordinate nephrotic treatment with glucose monitoring and adjustment (supported: Glucocorticoid decisions and diabetes risk must be managed together.) Diabetes means all nephrotic treatment should be abandoned (distractor: Comorbidity calls for adaptation rather than abandonment.) Copy historical steroid dosing without glucose monitoring (distractor: This ignores foreseeable metabolic risk.)
