## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What should be integrated beyond joint and menstrual complaints? Options: Immune-mediated hepatitis evidence and possible chronic-illness effects on the gonadal axis (supported: Marked liver enzymes, IgG and autoantibody findings require integration.) An isolated knee disorder explains all tests (distractor: It does not explain liver and immune abnormalities.) One low LH proves permanent ovarian failure (distractor: Axis level, illness effects and longitudinal data matter.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Immune tests, exclusions and histology jointly support AIH, with limited tissue sampling (supported: Neither ANA alone nor limited tissue completes all diagnostic assessment.) Positive ANA automatically means lupus hepatitis (distractor: Full systemic evidence is required.) AIH is proven to be the sole cause of amenorrhoea (distractor: A complete amenorrhoea differential is unavailable.)
- **management:** How should treatment and puberty follow-up be coordinated? Options: Hepatology/endocrinology should review diagnosis, immunotherapy safety and reproductive axis while protecting adolescent privacy (supported: Do not copy historical steroid pulses; follow function longitudinally.) Withdrawal bleeding proves spontaneous ovulation (distractor: Withdrawal bleeding differs from spontaneous regular cycles.) Resolved joint pain permits ending liver follow-up (distractor: Hepatic activity and symptoms may differ.)
