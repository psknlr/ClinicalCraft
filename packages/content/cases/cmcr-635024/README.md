## Clinical summary

Early onset, leanness, hyperandrogenic features and severe resistance support evaluation of a specific diabetes subtype, while candidate INSR variant function remains uncertain. Course and treatment response can justify reclassification. A candidate genetic change is not validated pathogenicity. Hypoglycaemia assessment must cover the night.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should leanness, early onset and an unusual insulin response affect classification? Options: Reassess severe insulin resistance and specific diabetes subtypes (supported: Phenotype, family clues and treatment response warrant revisiting the original label.) Conclude non-adherence from hyperglycaemia alone (distractor: Observed inpatient treatment and day-night differences require biological assessment.) Young age and earlier ketonuria exclude all other subtypes (distractor: Neither feature has absolute subtype specificity.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: The phenotype supports the reported type A resistance assessment, but variant causality remains incompletely established (supported: Candidate sequence findings and functional validation are different evidence levels.) Any detected variant proves the sole cause (distractor: The report explicitly called for functional work.) Ovarian changes alone explain all extreme resistance and complications (distractor: Hyperandrogenism can form part of a broader syndrome and should not end classification.)
- **management:** How should simultaneous hyperglycaemia and nocturnal hypoglycaemia be addressed? Options: Specialist review of administration, timing and classification, with attention to nocturnal hypoglycaemia and individual adjustment (supported: Dose escalation based only on daytime values ignores the full monitored pattern.) Copy the report’s extreme infusion doses as routine care (distractor: These historical exceptional interventions are not a transferable prescription.) Stop all diabetes follow-up permanently after nocturnal lows (distractor: Hyperglycaemia and serious organ complications still require specialist care.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Metabolic, antibody and complication assessment: Poor glycaemic control, hypoalbuminaemia, heavy proteinuria and dyslipidaemia; IAA, ICA and GADA were negative. Subsequent renal biopsy showed diabetic nephropathy and ocular assessment severe proliferative retinopathy.
- Monitoring and insulin-response record: Daytime glucose remained high despite escalating insulin, whereas nocturnal hypoglycaemia occurred. During observed withdrawal of insulin and oral agents glucose did not rise markedly further.
- Resistance work-up and genetic testing: Insulin and receptor antibodies were negative; thyroid/adrenal function and IGF-1 were described as normal. Testosterone and LH/FSH were elevated with mildly enlarged ovaries. INSR sequencing found several changes; an exon 21 stop change was considered potentially relevant, with functional significance requiring investigation.

**病例概要：** 早发、消瘦、高雄激素表型和严重胰岛素抵抗支持特殊类型糖尿病评估，候选INSR变异功能仍不确定。

**学习目标：** 辨认需要重新分型的非典型糖尿病特征；将高雄激素、消瘦与严重抵抗联系；区分候选基因发现与功能证实；提出覆盖夜间低血糖的安全评估
