## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should the liver lesion and near-obstructing primary be interpreted? Options: Metastatic rectal cancer requires MDT assessment of potential resectability and obstruction risk (supported: Tissue and imaging jointly guide treatment goals.) Young age justifies dismissing malignant biopsy (distractor: Age does not override tissue evidence.) - **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Partial response creates a reassessment opportunity but does not establish cure (supported: Radiological shrinkage still requires local-treatment and systemic reassessment.) A CEA fall proves cure (distractor: Markers do not replace complete tumour assessment.) Wild-type KRAS supplies all modern molecular information (distractor: Extended RAS and MMR/MSI are not documented.)
- **management:** How should care be discussed after a response? Options: Promptly reassess surgery, explore patient concerns and explain obstruction warnings and molecular gaps (supported: Respect informed choice while explaining risks and follow-up.) Blame the patient for declining surgery and stop follow-up (distractor: Explore concerns, share decisions and maintain care.) Fever with obstruction can wait for routine chemotherapy (distractor: Acute obstruction or abscess needs urgent surgical assessment.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Colonoscopy and biopsy: A friable bleeding mass 15 cm from the anus occupied about four fifths of the lumen and prevented scope passage; biopsy showed rectal adenocarcinoma.
- Initial staging imaging: PET-CT showed perirectal fat involvement, regional/retroperitoneal nodes and a roughly 10 cm liver lesion; the source labelled cT4bNxM1.
- Tumour and molecular records: CEA 302.40 ng/mL; KRAS and BRAF were reported wild-type, without full RAS or MMR/MSI details.
- Post-conversion assessment: After four reported cycles of FOLFIRI plus cetuximab, CEA and lesion size decreased; MDT assessed partial response and recommended resection, not pathological complete response.
