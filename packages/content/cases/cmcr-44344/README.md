## Learning outcomes

1. Explain evaluation of unexplained small-bowel bleeding.
2. Distinguish nuclear investigation purposes.
3. Prioritise severe anaemia.
4. Integrate imaging and tissue findings.

## Clinical summary

A jejunal cavernous haemangioma explained the chronic small-bowel bleeding. Negative routine endoscopy does not establish a normal entire small bowel. Different nuclear studies are not interchangeable. Histological classification complements imaging localisation.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should localisation be interpreted after negative conventional investigations? Options: A small-bowel lesion may lie beyond previous routine endoscopy and localisation must be paired with severe-bleeding care (supported: Negative tests depend on reach and timing; haemoglobin 28 g/L cannot be ignored.) Negative routine endoscopy excludes a gastrointestinal source (distractor: The small bowel remains insufficiently assessed.) Any positive nuclear focus proves Meckel diverticulum (distractor: Labelled-red-cell and ectopic-mucosa studies answer different questions.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Surgical pathology classifies the localised lesion as a bleeding cavernous haemangioma (supported: Enhancement and calcification were clues; tissue provides classification in this case.) This case proves CT is 100% accurate for all bleeding (distractor: A single case cannot establish universal accuracy.) Iron-deficiency marrow means bleeding originates in marrow (distractor: Iron deficiency reflects chronic blood loss rather than its intestinal location.)
- **management:** How should a safe modern plan be framed? Options: Assess and stabilise severe blood-loss risk, then plan lesion-directed evaluation and treatment with specialists (supported: Further localisation must not delay severe-anaemia support; paediatric methods require individualisation.) Continue old remedies without addressing haemoglobin (distractor: Prior benefit was poor and anaemia is extreme.) Apply historical radiotherapy advice to every diffuse lesion (distractor: Historical discussion is not a current paediatric vascular-anomaly protocol.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Blood count and blood-loss assessment: Haemoglobin 28 g/L, red cells 1.81×10^12/L and platelets 363×10^9/L; stool occult blood ++, normal coagulation and an iron-deficiency marrow pattern.
- Radionuclide and contrast CT localisation: Ectopic gastric mucosa imaging was negative; labelled-red-cell imaging showed a relatively fixed focus. CT found a roughly 2.0×3.5 cm jejunal mass with marked portal-venous enhancement and punctate calcification.
- Surgery and pathology: A 5×3×3 cm dark-red nodular mass lay about 5 cm distal to the ligament of Treitz; resection showed cavernous haemangioma with haemorrhage and calcification.

## 中文摘要

**病例概要：** 长期小肠出血最终由空肠海绵状血管瘤解释。

**学习目标：** 解释不明原因小肠出血路径。；区分不同核素研究目的。；识别严重贫血的优先级。；整合影像和组织结果。

**关键决策：** 多次常规检查阴性后应怎样解释本次定位？ 后续资料能支持哪一项结论？ 怎样表述安全的现代计划？

**复盘要点：** 常规内镜阴性不代表全小肠正常；不同核素检查不能互换解释；病理定性与影像定位互补。尚待核对：缺少长期复发、现代小肠内镜及血管异常分类复核。本稿尚未经执业医师审核。
