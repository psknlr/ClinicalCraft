## Learning outcomes

After the case the learner can:

1. Recognise suspected small-bowel bleeding when black stools recur after negative gastroscopy, colonoscopy and CT, and
   plan the small-bowel work-up — capsule endoscopy first-line when no narrowing is suspected, CT enterography, and a
   pertechnetate scan in a child (ESGE 2022; ACG 2015).
2. Manage chronic severe anaemia (haemoglobin 28 g/L) in a compensated child: weight-based red-cell aliquots run
   slowly with reassessment for circulatory overload after each, then iron replacement (NICE NG24, 2015; BSH 2016).
3. Interpret localisation studies by what each can show: a negative ectopic gastric mucosa scan, a relatively fixed
   focus on a labelled red-cell scan, and a jejunal mass with portal-venous enhancement and punctate calcification on
   CT.
4. Plan definitive treatment of a solitary bleeding small-bowel vascular lesion — segmental resection with primary
   anastomosis once the anaemia is corrected, with the whole small bowel examined — and explain why radiotherapy,
   propranolol or endoscopic ablation are not the answer (ESGE 2022; AAP 2019).
5. Relate the pathology label "cavernous haemangioma" to the ISSVA classification (2018) of vascular tumours and
   malformations, and plan follow-up for iron repletion, recurrence and multifocal disease accordingly.
6. Explain the reasons for and risks of transfusion and surgery to a 12-year-old and his family, seeking his assent and
   their informed consent, and say what the record cannot answer.

These are the case's `learningOutcomes` (shown before play only on request; listed in every debrief). The six HUD
`objectives` below are the in-game task list.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

**Presentation.** A 12-year-old boy has had black stools and pallor on and off for eight years. They began after a
low-grade fever, with intermittent periumbilical pain; they have recurred since, and again over the last month.
Gastroscopy, colonoscopy and abdominal CT at several hospitals found no cause; intermittent omeprazole and Yunnan Baiyao
helped little. He has been told he bleeds in his gut, but never why.

**Admission.** He looks severely anaemic; heart rate 100/min; a grade II/6 systolic murmur over the precordium; abdomen
soft, liver and spleen not palpable, no leg oedema. Haemoglobin **28 g/L**, red cells 1.81×10¹²/L, platelets
363×10⁹/L; faecal occult blood **++**; clotting normal; the bone marrow shows an iron-deficiency pattern. No other vital
sign, no red-cell index, ferritin, weight or height is recorded.

**Localisation.** The ectopic gastric mucosa (Meckel) scan is **negative**; a labelled red-cell scan shows a
**relatively fixed focus** of tracer (its site is not recorded); contrast-enhanced CT shows a **jejunal mass of about
2.0 × 3.5 cm** that **enhances markedly in the portal venous phase**, with **punctate calcification**.

**Treatment and outcome.** The anaemia was **corrected first** (how is not recorded). At operation a **dark-red nodular
mass of 5 × 3 × 3 cm** lay **about 5 cm distal to the ligament of Treitz**; a **partial jejunal resection with
anastomosis** was done. Histology: **cavernous haemangioma with haemorrhage and calcification**. He recovered well
after the operation; nothing later is recorded.

**Diagnosis.** Chronic, recurrent small-bowel bleeding from a solitary cavernous haemangioma of the proximal jejunum —
in ISSVA (2018) terms most likely a slow-flow venous malformation (the case cannot confirm the reclassification: no
immunohistochemistry is recorded) — with severe iron-deficiency anaemia.

**Today's answer.** Correct the anaemia safely — slow, weight-based red-cell aliquots with reassessment for
circulatory overload (NICE NG24, 2015; BSH 2016), then iron; treat the bleeding as suspected small-bowel bleeding and
search the small bowel (capsule endoscopy first-line when no narrowing is suspected, cross-sectional enterography, a
pertechnetate scan in a child; ESGE 2022, ACG 2015); resect a solitary lesion once the anaemia is corrected, examining
the whole small bowel; name the lesion in ISSVA terms and follow up for iron repletion, recurrence and multifocal
disease. The recorded course — anaemia corrected first, then resection — matches today's order; the localisation used
nuclear scans and CT, and no capsule endoscopy or enteroscopy is recorded.

### Decisions

- **1 `anaemia-plan`** (single; needs this bout's story, the six examination findings and the blood count, faecal
  occult blood and clotting in phase `admission`). Key: `slow-aliquots` — crossmatch and transfuse red cells slowly in
  weight-based aliquots, reassessing for circulatory overload after each, then iron. Distractors:
  `rapid-full-correction` (20 mL/kg over an hour, repeated to a normal Hb), `iv-iron-alone` (no transfusion while
  "compensated"), `oral-iron-home` (discharge with clinic tests), `operate-first` (exploratory laparotomy today,
  transfusing in theatre).
- **2 `source-search`** (select all; needs decision 1, onset, earlier tests and medicines asked, and the earlier
  hospitals' reports read). Keys: `pertechnetate-scan`, `ct-enterography`, `capsule-if-no-narrowing`. Distractors:
  `repeat-scopes-only` (another gastroscopy and colonoscopy, stopping if normal), `red-cell-scan-first` (a labelled
  red-cell scan as the first localising test — the report's own test, used here as the historical near-miss).
- **3 `bleeding-source`** (single; needs decision 2 and the three localisation records in phase `localisation`). Key:
  `jejunal-vascular-lesion` — a vascular lesion of the jejunum such as a cavernous haemangioma (a venous malformation),
  bleeding on and off. Distractors: `missed-meckel`, `jejunal-gist` (the near-miss: only histology separates them),
  `jejunal-lymphoma`, `diffuse-angioectasia`.
- **4 `definitive-treatment`** (single; needs decision 3). Key: `resection-after-correction` — once the anaemia is
  corrected, resect the affected jejunal segment with a primary anastomosis and check the whole small bowel.
  Distractors: `enteroscopic-coagulation` (APC through a double-balloon enteroscope), `propranolol-course`,
  `radiotherapy-course` (the historical treatment of haemangiomas), `sirolimus-course`.
- **5 `after-surgery-plan`** (select all; needs decision 4, the operation note and the pathology report). Keys:
  `iron-until-replete`, `look-for-other-lesions`, `return-if-recurs`. Distractors: `propranolol-prevention`,
  `six-monthly-ct`.

Decisions are scored with `decidedFirstTry`, so guessing through the options costs the item. The four harmful
interventions (rapid transfusion, discharge home, radiotherapy, propranolol) are always `contraindicated`, and the
resection is contraindicated while no transfusion has been given — each named in the debrief's safety list with its

**Timing of the critical act.** `rb-transfusion` counts only a transfusion given while its `indicatedWhen` held (blood
count read, phase `admission` or `localisation`), so a transfusion after the operation records does not earn it; the
localisation phase itself waits for an anaemia act. **Known limits:** the rubric's conditions carry no time beyond
that window, so transfusion observations, consent, iron and the follow-up plan given late still meet their
non-critical items; debrief the order of the acts from the transcript.
