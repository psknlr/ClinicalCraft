## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should lung and urine evidence change the diagnostic direction? Options: Consider a pulmonary–renal syndrome and small-vessel vasculitis while continuing infection assessment (supported: Blood-streaked sputum, anaemia, urinary changes and rising creatinine require an explanation beyond lung imaging alone.) An initially negative ANCA excludes small-vessel vasculitis (distractor: Negative serology alone cannot negate multi-system evidence and requires contextual reassessment.) Afternoon low fever fixes the diagnosis as tuberculosis (distractor: The symptom is non-specific and treatment failed to help, requiring reintegration of renal and blood findings.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Later pANCA supports the clinical direction but is not MPO confirmation or histology (supported: Titre, antigen specificity and histology are distinct evidence types.) Treatment response can be recorded as biopsy confirmation (distractor: Biopsy was not performed because of risk; pathology cannot be invented.) Repeated negative cultures exclude every infection (distractor: Sampling, prior therapy and testing scope still limit negative findings.)
- **management:** When biopsy risk is high, how should diagnosis and treatment safety be handled? Options: Balance organ threat, biopsy risk and infection in a multidisciplinary plan, explicitly stating limits of the clinical diagnosis and monitoring (supported: The report used clinical evidence, not pathological confirmation; both delay and immunosuppression risks require assessment.) Permanently reject systemic assessment without a biopsy (distractor: Infeasible biopsy does not prevent integrated clinical assessment, especially with threatened organs.) Base all treatment solely on ANCA titres (distractor: Serological titres do not replace assessment of organ injury, infection and toxicity.)

**学习目标：** 以肺肾证据建立血管炎鉴别方向；解释初次ANCA阴性的局限；辨别临床诊断与组织确证；提出兼顾感染及器官威胁的监测计划
