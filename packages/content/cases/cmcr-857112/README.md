## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise high-pressure low-volume ventilation emergencies.
2. Interpret positional changes in relation to tube position.
3. Distinguish transient response from causal confirmation.
4. Explain the safety role of trained airway teams.

## Clinical summary

The report attributed recurrent ventilation failure to a displaced tracheostomy tube abutting the tracheal wall. Pressure and exhaled volume jointly indicate impaired ventilation. Repeated positional pressure changes provide a localising clue. Transient bronchodilator response does not exclude a mechanical problem.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should high airway pressure, low ventilation and severe hypoxaemia be framed? Options: An airway or ventilation emergency requiring assessment for obstruction, displacement and bronchospasm (supported: High pressure with low exhaled volume indicates impaired ventilation; chronic lung disease does not replace airway assessment.) Established asthma requiring repeated nebulisation alone (distractor: Asthma was not documented and severe hypoventilation requires urgent evaluation for mechanical causes.) High blood pressure means anxiety alone (distractor: Hypoxaemia, hypercapnia and convulsions cannot be explained by anxiety alone.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Position-dependent pressure changes support tube-position obstruction without direct endoscopic proof (supported: The repeated positional association supports a mechanism without inventing bronchoscopy findings.) A response to nebulisation excludes mechanical obstruction (distractor: Transient improvement and relapse do not exclude coexisting mechanical irritation or obstruction.) A non-elevated white count confirms tube infection (distractor: This neither confirms infection nor explains strong positional dependence.)
- **management:** What is the safe present-day lesson from this historical event? Options: Call an experienced airway team and assess patency and position using a tracheostomy emergency algorithm (supported: Critical hypoxaemia requires immediate action; trained teams assess and manage tube adjustment or removal.) Continue daily nebulisation and wait five days before considering tube position (distractor: This converts historical delay into a protocol and may prolong severe hypoxaemia.) Ask family to repeatedly advance the tube blindly (distractor: Blind advancement risks injury and false passage and is not safe teaching.)

**学习目标：** 识别高压力低通气的气道急症。；解释体位依赖与管位问题的关系。；区分短时药物反应与病因确认。；说明受训气道团队的安全职责。

**关键决策：** 面对高气道压、低通气量和重度低氧，首先应怎样归纳问题？ 后续资料能支持哪一项结论？ 如何把这一历史事件转化为当前安全教学？
