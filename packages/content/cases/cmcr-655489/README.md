## Learning outcomes

1. Retain small-bowel bleeding after negative standard endoscopy
2. Connect anaemia, melaena and imaging localisation
3. Interpret the vascular lesion using histology
4. Propose planning that balances bleeding control with preserved bowel function

## Clinical summary

Operative histology of small-bowel and mesenteric cavernous haemangioma explained recurrent small-bowel blood loss. Negative standard endoscopy does not exclude small-bowel disease. The nature of a cystic mass requires pathological corroboration. Bleeding control and preservation of absorptive function must be considered together.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What reasoning follows negative standard endoscopy with ongoing melaena and severe anaemia? Options: Investigate a small-bowel source and relate the mass to bleeding (supported: Standard endoscopy does not cover the whole small bowel, and imaging provides new localisation.) Negative endoscopy excludes gastrointestinal bleeding (distractor: Melaena, strongly positive occult blood and severe anaemia remain objective evidence.) Consider only marrow production failure (distractor: Marrow findings were unremarkable while recurrent melaena supports blood loss.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Histology confirms a bowel-wall/mesenteric vascular lesion explaining recurrent occult loss (supported: Thin-walled vessels and lesion extent connect morphology with bleeding.) Absence of skin lesions excludes an intestinal vascular lesion (distractor: No skin lesion cannot negate histologically proven intestinal disease.) Cystic imaging proves an unrelated simple cyst (distractor: Imaging shape is not equivalent to histological identity.)
- **management:** What safe plan follows severe anaemia and extensive disease? Options: Joint paediatric gastroenterology/surgical assessment of bleeding stability, extent and bowel preservation (supported: Control of blood loss must be balanced against nutritional harm from extensive resection.) Ignore bowel compression and prescribe capsule investigation unconditionally (distractor: Compression requires specialist patency and modality assessment rather than mechanical use of a general pathway.) Ignore current severe anaemia because earlier episodes improved (distractor: Temporary improvement did not prevent recurrence and major blood loss.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Blood count and faecal occult blood: Haemoglobin 45 g/L, red cells 1.90 × 10^12/L and occult blood ++++; white cells 6.1 × 10^9/L and platelets 207 × 10^9/L.
- Endoscopy and abdominal imaging: Upper endoscopy, colonoscopy and isotope scanning were unremarkable; contrast imaging showed an approximately 8 × 7 cm mass compressing small bowel, ultrasound an 11.0 × 7.9 × 4.0 cm honeycomb cystic lesion, and CT a cystic abdominopelvic mass.
- Operative and resection pathology record: Multiple small-bowel and mesenteric lesions led to approximately 70 cm resection; dilated thin-walled vessels traversed bowel layers, some filled with red cells, reported as cavernous haemangioma.
