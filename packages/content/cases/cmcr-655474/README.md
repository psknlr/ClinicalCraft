## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise destructive red flags in persistent bone pain.
2. Use immunophenotype to distinguish histiocytic disorders.
3. Identify molecular and organ-staging gaps.
4. Discuss treatment within current evidence limits.

## Clinical summary

The source classified the tissue and immunophenotype as paediatric Erdheim–Chester disease. Multifocal bone destruction exceeds an ordinary joint-pain explanation. Tissue and immunohistochemistry are key classification evidence. Missing molecular and organ staging limits treatment decisions.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What investigation direction follows from the pain and early records? Options: Multifocal bone lesions require tissue-based differentiation rather than treatment as growing pains (supported: Vertebral and iliac destruction provide important structural evidence.) Normal joint spaces establish growing pains (distractor: Normal joint spaces do not explain destructive bone lesions.) CRP elevation alone proves septic arthritis (distractor: Distribution and normal joint spaces do not support that conclusion alone.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Foamy histiocytes and phenotype support a non-Langerhans histiocytosis, integrated with imaging and molecular evaluation (supported: CD68 positivity with CD1a/S100 negativity fits the reported interpretation, without supplied molecular results.) Negative CD1a confirms Langerhans cell histiocytosis (distractor: This reverses the relevant immunophenotypic distinction.) BRAF V600E is proven positive (distractor: The source did not report BRAF testing.)
- **management:** How should further management be discussed today? Options: Refer to histiocytosis specialists for organ staging and molecular assessment before individualised treatment (supported: The 2020 consensus supports tissue and molecular assessment; rare paediatric cases require specialist judgement.) Poor historical results mean no treatment options exist today (distractor: Advances in molecular treatment make that inference outdated.) Give every child the same targeted drug without staging (distractor: Organ involvement, molecular findings and paediatric suitability affect selection.)
