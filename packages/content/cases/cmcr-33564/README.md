## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which findings require looking beyond ordinary obesity-related diabetes? Options: A childhood-onset syndromic insulin-receptor disorder (supported: Growth, skin, dental, tongue and genital findings need a unifying explanation.) Pubertal obesity alone explains all findings (distractor: Early onset and growth failure are not accounted for.) Absence of polyuria excludes dysglycaemia (distractor: Glucose and HbA1c are abnormal.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Phenotype and variants inherited from different parents support the Rabson–Mendenhall spectrum (supported: Segregation strengthens interpretation, although functional studies are unavailable.) Normal parental phenotypes exclude inherited disease (distractor: Recessive carriers may have normal phenotypes.) One insulin value establishes long-term secretory capacity (distractor: Glucose, course and longitudinal assessment are needed.)
- **management:** What is a safe follow-up plan? Options: Endocrine and genetic teams should assess glycaemic risk, growth and family support (supported: Avoid extrapolated self-treatment and address the impact of teasing.) No thirst means glucose monitoring is unnecessary (distractor: Absent symptoms do not negate biochemical disease.) Genetic confirmation means a curative drug is established (distractor: The report supplies no such treatment evidence.)
