## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What argues against nephrotic syndrome as the sole explanation? Options: Marked weakness, characteristic rash and high CK support inflammatory myopathy (supported: Oedema is not specific for nephrosis; compare urine and muscle evidence.) All generalised oedema is nephrotic syndrome (distractor: Proteinuria evidence is lacking and myopathy is prominent.) Fever explains all prolonged weakness (distractor: Rash and myopathic evidence need their own explanation.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Histology and clinical features support JDM while infection and systemic vascular risks remain relevant (supported: Histological wording and clinical subtype are not mechanically identical.) A Candida culture alone proves invasive pulmonary candidiasis (distractor: Clinical and tissue evidence are needed.) A short-term CK fall excludes serious later complications (distractor: Muscle enzymes do not represent every organ/vascular risk.)
- **management:** What is safer with severe myositis and concurrent infection? Options: Rheumatology/critical-care teams should balance immunotherapy and infection and assess swallowing, respiration and new abdominal/bleeding warnings (supported: New abdominal pain must not be dismissed as ordinary gastritis.) Treat pain only with acid suppression until major bleeding occurs (distractor: Severe JDM may involve critical gastrointestinal disease requiring earlier escalation.) Ignore infection and repeat historical steroid pulses (distractor: Disease and treatment risks require individual balancing.)
