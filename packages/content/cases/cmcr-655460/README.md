## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise neonatal necrotic purpura as an emergency.
2. Interpret consumptive coagulation patterns.
3. Explain replacement effects on testing.
4. Retain diagnostic limits without delaying emergency care for genetics.

## Clinical summary

The source clinically diagnosed neonatal purpura fulminans associated with congenital protein C deficiency; the molecular genotype was not established. Rapid necrosis and consumptive coagulopathy support each other. Post-replacement levels are not untreated baseline values. Family data support suspicion without replacing genetic confirmation.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What do necrotic skin changes and coagulation abnormalities suggest? Options: Purpura-fulminans-like microvascular thrombosis with consumptive coagulopathy requiring urgent assessment (supported: Palpable pedal pulses do not exclude microvascular thrombosis; coagulation abnormalities suggest a systemic process.) A strong cry proves a local bruise alone (distractor: Rapid necrosis and low fibrinogen are not ordinary bruising.) Palpable arteries exclude all ischaemia (distractor: Large-artery patency does not exclude microvascular injury.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Results support a clinical suspicion of protein C pathway deficiency, limited by post-plasma sampling and age; genotype is unproven (supported: Family levels support further investigation without establishing a homozygous or compound-heterozygous genotype.) 22% proves complete normality and ends evaluation (distractor: Testing followed replacement and the severe phenotype still needs explanation.) Biallelic PROC mutations have been identified (distractor: No molecular testing was supplied.)
- **management:** How should a safe current plan be stated? Options: Urgent neonatal and haematology collaboration to assess replacement support for severe deficiency and coagulopathy without waiting for genetics (supported: Protein C concentrate has an indication for purpura fulminans in severe congenital deficiency; specialists determine the regimen.) Wait for genetics before treating progressing necrosis (distractor: Progressive lesions and coagulopathy cannot await molecular confirmation.) Stop all support after temporary skin improvement (distractor: Temporary improvement does not establish resolution of the underlying process.)
