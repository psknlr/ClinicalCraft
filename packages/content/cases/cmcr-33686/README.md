## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should infection and uncontrolled inflammation be interpreted? Options: Multiple HLH features with EBV as a possible trigger warrant genetic-susceptibility assessment (supported: Infection does not exclude coexisting immune dysregulation.) EBV positivity permits observing this as ordinary viral fever (distractor: Cytopenias, low fibrinogen and inflammation indicate greater risk.) Any haemophagocyte alone establishes HLH (distractor: A clinical/laboratory combination is required.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Genetic assessment is warranted, but unclear nomenclature cannot establish a unique FHL3 subtype (supported: Heterozygous UNC13D and another gene signal need specialist review.) One heterozygous UNC13D variant fully proves recessive FHL3 (distractor: A second allele and sufficient functional/family evidence are lacking.) No family history excludes hereditary HLH (distractor: A negative pedigree does not exclude inherited disease.)
- **management:** How should care be organised when transplantation is considered? Options: HLH/transplant teams should control active disease, verify genetics/donor choice and distinguish infection from GVHD (supported: Historical conditioning or donor details are not an individual prescription.) Normal blood genotype after transplant proves whole-body genetic repair (distractor: Donor haematopoiesis changes blood testing, not every tissue genotype.) Post-transplant diarrhoea can only be infection (distractor: Infection and GVHD may coexist or occur sequentially.)
