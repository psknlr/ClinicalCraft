## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What reassessment is required by deterioration after delivery? Options: Thrombotic microangiopathy, including TTP and other pregnancy-associated TMAs (supported: Severe thrombocytopenia, neurological injury and haemolysis clues require more than attribution to the completed pregnancy.) Delivery guarantees spontaneous resolution of all abnormalities (distractor: Continued deterioration requires reassessment.) Isolated immune thrombocytopenia explains everything (distractor: It does not adequately explain ischaemia, schistocytes and LDH elevation.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: TMA is strongly suspected; the report labels TTP without ADAMTS13 subtype confirmation (supported: Retain the confirmation boundary between TTP and other TMAs.) Schistocytes specifically prove immune TTP (distractor: They support microangiopathic haemolysis, not one cause.) An initially normal CT excludes later cerebral ischaemia (distractor: Later MRI documented ischaemia; timing and modality differ.)
- **management:** What should be prioritised when reviewing this high-risk combination today? Options: Urgent haematology/critical-care review, prompt ADAMTS13 sampling and emergency suspected-TTP assessment including plasma exchange; avoid routine prophylactic platelet transfusion (supported: Life-threatening TMA should not await complete confirmation; bleeding or urgent procedures require individual assessment.) Repeated platelet transfusion to normalise the count is the sole goal (distractor: This neglects the microthrombotic process and may be unsafe.) Wait another week to see whether delivery works (distractor: Neurological and organ deterioration require immediate escalation.)
