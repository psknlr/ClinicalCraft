## Clinical summary

Surgery and pathology established extralobar pulmonary sequestration associated with the persistent local mass and pleural effusion. Prenatal onset suggests a congenital cause. Descending-aortic supply is a key anatomical clue. Separate encapsulation and lung tissue establish classification.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What does recurrent unilateral fluid with a local mass require? Options: Evaluate a congenital local structural lesion and its supply rather than drainage alone (supported: Prenatal onset, a local mass and persistence make a structural cause important.) Recurrent fluid proves inadequate antibiotics (distractor: Pathogen evidence is lacking and the solid lesion remains unexplained.) Treat as congenital heart failure in every case (distractor: Cardiac structure/function were reported normal without generalised oedema.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Systemic supply, separate encapsulation and lung tissue support extralobar pulmonary sequestration (supported: These complementary findings classify the lesion beyond the effusion alone.) Any pleural fluid establishes a simple cyst (distractor: Abnormal supply and pulmonary tissue were documented.) A negative chyle test during fasting excludes all lymphatic disease (distractor: Feeding status affects chylous appearances and prevents absolute exclusion.)
- **management:** What does this evidence imply for safe management? Options: Neonatal and paediatric surgical teams should integrate respiratory support with vascular anatomy in planning (supported: Aberrant systemic arterial supply affects bleeding risk and operative planning.) Puncture the mass as a simple cyst (distractor: Puncture without recognising anomalous vessels risks serious injury.) Continue aspiration alone while ignoring mass and supply (distractor: Persistence and ventilator dependence demand aetiological management.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Pleural fluid and pathogen records: Fluid cells 260×10^6/L, 85% mononuclear, protein 9.2 g/L; chyle test and cultures negative. Prolonged fasting limits interpretation of a negative chyle test.
- Early chest and systemic assessment: Ultrasound showed left fluid with a solid echo; CT demonstrated a left lower thoracic mass and rightward mediastinal displacement. Cardiac structure/function were normal, albumin 35.3 g/L and CRP <1 mg/L.
- Contrast CT and surgical pathology: Contrast CT showed an arterial branch from the descending aorta supplying the lesion. Surgery found a separately encapsulated 5.5×4×3 cm structure with a vascular pedicle, disconnected from normal lung; histology contained alveolar and bronchial structures.
