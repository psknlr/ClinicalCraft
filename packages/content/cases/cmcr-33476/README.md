## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What source should be prioritised for cerebral and peripheral events? Options: An embolic left-heart lesion with associated obstruction (supported: A mobile left atrial mass provides a plausible shared source for multiple arterial events.) Isolated cerebral disease without peripheral-perfusion assessment (distractor: Absent pulses and cool limbs are separate urgent findings.) Absence of a murmur excludes a cardiac cause (distractor: Direct echocardiographic structural evidence is not negated by absent murmur.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Histology establishes tumour type but not that every embolus was tumour tissue (supported: Surface thrombus and tumour fragments are both possible; individual emboli were not sampled.) All venous and arterial clots are histologically proven tumour (distractor: That sampling is absent, and venous and arterial mechanisms need distinction.) Improved pulmonary oedema means the intracardiac mass is risk-free (distractor: Intermittent obstruction and repeat embolic risk may persist.)
- **management:** How should cerebral and limb risks be balanced next? Options: Urgent cardiac-surgical, neurological and vascular assessment of mass treatment timing, cerebral bleeding risk and limb perfusion (supported: A fixed anticoagulant dose or historical delay cannot replace individual risk assessment.) Copy the report’s several-week surgical delay without reassessment (distractor: Multiple emboli and obstruction create evolving risk requiring team judgement.) Anticoagulation permanently replaces management of the mass (distractor: Anticoagulation cannot remove the tumour or mechanical obstruction.)
