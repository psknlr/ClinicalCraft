## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise DKA evidence.
2. Distinguish compensatory breathing from air leakage.
3. Interpret perforation assessment.
4. Discuss individualised intervention and metabolic monitoring.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should deep breathing and crepitus be integrated? Options: DKA coexists with evidence of thoracic air leakage; acidosis cannot explain all respiratory findings (supported: Subcutaneous crepitus requires separate imaging assessment.) DKA explains crepitus without chest assessment (distractor: Acidosis does not replace localisation of air leakage.) Chest signs exclude a metabolic emergency (distractor: Hyperglycaemia, ketosis and acidosis are all present.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Pneumomediastinum is documented without an obvious tear, while dangerous causes still require symptom-based assessment (supported: Negative reports reduce some possibilities without proving absolute absence at every time.) Coffee-ground emesis alone proves oesophageal rupture (distractor: It indicates bleeding rather than specific proof of perforation.) This DKA episode alone determines lifelong diabetes type (distractor: Classification requires later endocrine evidence whose detailed values are incomplete.)
- **management:** How should this combination be managed safely? Options: Use monitored adult DKA care and specialist assessment of air-leak severity and intervention need (supported: One report’s cervical drainage or acid correction cannot become mandatory for every case.) Every DKA-related pneumomediastinum requires cervical incision (distractor: Invasive management depends on actual severity and complications.) pH 7.10 mandates routine high-dose bicarbonate (distractor: The 2024 adult consensus does not support routine use without an indication.)
