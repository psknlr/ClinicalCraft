## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** What does poor response to repeated local surgery suggest? Options: Multilevel airway disease and systemic tissue growth may coexist beyond one pharyngeal lesion (supported: Appearance, pharyngeal enlargement and retrosternal compression require integration.) Previous surgery means the airway is normal (distractor: Severe breathlessness and hypoxaemia persist.) Weight management alone explains every abnormality (distractor: Pituitary and tissue findings cannot be ignored.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Phenotype, GH and pituitary imaging support the historical acromegaly diagnosis, with IGF-1 missing for modern biochemical review (supported: Unreported IGF-1 cannot be assumed normal or elevated.) Normal thyroid hormones exclude tracheal compression (distractor: Structural compression differs from hormonal function.) The old AHI is a complete current sleep study (distractor: The source explicitly says no complete current PSG.)
- **management:** How should the current safety pathway be stated? Options: Experienced airway/critical-care teams should stabilise breathing with coordinated pituitary, thyroid and sleep assessment (supported: Severe multilevel obstruction and hypoxaemia require coordinated planning.) All fixed airway compression requires only home CPAP (distractor: Critical compression may require specialist airway intervention.) Choose one conflicting postoperative story as fact (distractor: Operative and non-operative outcome accounts conflict and must be disclosed.)
