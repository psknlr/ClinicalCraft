## Clinical summary

Enzymatic and molecular findings support MT-ATP6 m.8993T>G-related complex V deficiency with a Leigh phenotype. Course and family history suggest a progressive cause. MRI and metabolic results provide complementary clues. The nucleotide finding is more traceable than conflicting historical annotation.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which clues require assessment beyond a static cerebral palsy label? Options: Progression, a similar sibling course, lactate and symmetrical deep-brain lesions suggest inherited metabolic disease (supported: Convergent evidence supports further assessment; normal routine screening does not exclude mitochondrial disease.) Asphyxial brain injury is established despite no birth asphyxia (distractor: The source does not support that cause and the course is progressive.) Normal screening excludes all genetic disease (distractor: Screening does not cover every mitochondrial function or genetic abnormality.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Enzyme and MT-ATP6 findings support complex-V-related Leigh spectrum disease; heteroplasmy does not guarantee an individual outcome (supported: Actual molecular evidence is present; an asymptomatic mother can carry the variant.) A negative father proves autosomal dominant inheritance (distractor: These are mitochondrial DNA findings and do not establish that pattern.) Every historical protein annotation should be copied (distractor: Current GeneReviews lists m.8993T>G as p.Leu156Arg, conflicting with the historical annotation.)
- **management:** What should current care prioritise? Options: Metabolic/genetic and neurological teams should address swallowing, respiration, seizures and family counselling (supported: Functional risks and family counselling matter; supplements cannot be promised to reverse the course.) Guarantee cure with vitamins and coenzyme (distractor: Neither this report nor the checked evidence supports that promise.) A healthy mother means the child needs no follow-up (distractor: Heteroplasmy and expression differ and the child has established impairment.)

## Clinical numbers check

- Metabolic and MRI record: Lactate 6.1 mmol/L, pyruvate 276 µmol/L and bicarbonate 21.7 mmol/L; routine amino-acid, acylcarnitine and urine organic-acid screening was normal. MRI showed symmetrical basal-ganglia and cerebral-peduncle abnormalities. Inconsistent source pyruvate reference units are not reproduced as a validated interval.
- Enzyme and mitochondrial DNA results: Leucocyte complex V activity was 28 nmol/(min·mg mitochondrial protein), with I–IV normal. Blood MT-ATP6 m.8993T>G heteroplasmy was approximately 90% in the child and 45% in the mother, and not detected in the father.
