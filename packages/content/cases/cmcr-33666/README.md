## Learning outcomes

After the case the learner can:

1. Tell reactive from primary thrombocytosis in a child — a count above 1000 × 10⁹/L that outlasts
   antibiotics, a CRP below 8 mg/L, a palpable spleen and a raised white count — and plan the film, iron
   studies, BCR::ABL1, driver mutations and marrow (GTH/GPOH 2021; BSH 2010).
2. Hold aspirin at an extreme platelet count until von Willebrand factor activity is known, explain acquired
   von Willebrand syndrome, and image the abdominal veins with Doppler ultrasound before treating abdominal pain
   as thrombosis (Tiede et al., 2011).
3. Apply the WHO 5th edition (2022) criteria to a JAK2 V617F-positive, BCR::ABL1-negative marrow and say what
   is still needed to separate essential thrombocythaemia from polycythaemia vera and prefibrotic myelofibrosis.
4. Choose, with paediatric haematology and from adult evidence (ELN 2018; Tefferi 2024; PT-1, 2005), one
   first-line cytoreductive drug for a young child — hydroxyurea or interferon-α, not both — and give the family
   specific advice on aspirin, Reye's syndrome, NSAIDs and vaccination.
5. Manage a new afebrile seizure in a child with a myeloproliferative neoplasm as a possible stroke or venous
   sinus thrombosis: glucose, count and clotting, antiplatelet drugs held, a same-day MRI with vascular
   sequences (RCPCH 2017).
6. Explain a long-term blood disorder to a child's family with Calgary–Cambridge chunking and teach-back,
   including what this report's course can and cannot show.

These are the case's `learningOutcomes`; they name the diagnosis, so front-ends show them before play only
behind the closed "Learning outcomes (6) · may reveal the diagnosis" disclosure and list them in every debrief
(CASE_AUTHORING §9.4, GLOSSARY §4). The six HUD `objectives` below are the in-game task list.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

A previously healthy 5-year-old girl, whose growth and development match other children her age, had ten days
of intermittent periumbilical pain with no obvious trigger; over the same period a blood test showed a very
high platelet count. IV anti-infective treatment before admission eased the pain a little but did not change
the blood count materially. She had no fever or rhinorrhoea, no apparent mucosal bleeding, and no nausea,
vomiting, diarrhoea or constipation. No family history of inherited disease is reported.

**On examination:** alert and generally well; no petechiae, bruises or bleeding spots; a soft abdomen tender
around the umbilicus without rebound; spleen palpable 2 cm below the costal margin. No vital signs are
recorded.

**Results:** two counts, in this order — platelets 1905 then 1497 × 10⁹/L, white cells 27.29 then
20.27 × 10⁹/L (dates not recorded); CRP < 8 mg/L; PT 14.2 s, APTT 52.3 s, fibrinogen 1.84 g/L (no reference
ranges recorded); no VWF test. Marrow smear: 172 megakaryocytes, 100 of them platelet-producing; trephine:
myeloproliferative changes, reticulin "±". JAK2 at V617: heterozygous G/T (JAK2 V617F); BCR::ABL1 P210 and a
second fusion test negative; karyotype 46,XX. The report's diagnosis: **childhood essential thrombocythaemia
with onset in the preschool years**; its JAK2 result (G/T at V617) is the V617F mutation.

**Course:** treatment included hydroxyurea, interferon and aspirin, among others (doses, start days and order not
recorded). On treatment day 2 she had a brief afebrile seizure; a contrast-enhanced MRI showed no abnormality.
Platelets were 559 × 10⁹/L by day 15 and 220–390 × 10⁹/L over 5 months of follow-up, and the abdominal pain
did not return.

**Today's answer:** look for a primary cause once an extreme count outlasts the treatment of a presumed infection;
measure the bleeding risk (VWF activity, the APTT against its range) and image the abdominal veins before any
antiplatelet drug or anticoagulant; complete the WHO 2022 criteria (haemoglobin, a marrow morphology review
against prefibrotic myelofibrosis, given her white count and spleen); lower the count with one first-line
cytoreductive drug, hydroxyurea or interferon-α, chosen with paediatric haematology and the family (interferon
spares decades of hydroxyurea but means weekly injections and flu-like and mood effects at 5), with aspirin only
once VWF activity is shown to be adequate (ristocetin cofactor activity above 30 %); give the family specific advice on aspirin, Reye's syndrome, NSAIDs and vaccines; and
treat a new seizure as a possible stroke or sinus thrombosis with same-day imaging.

**Critical items are acts**, not decisions: `rb-no-antithrombotic` (the dangerous error: an antiplatelet drug or
an anticoagulant before the bleeding risk and a clot are known) and `rb-seizure-imaging` (the missed
life-saving act). Timing is enforced the way the pilot cases enforce theirs: `obj-first-steps`, which opens
`work-up`, needs the VWF tests and the Doppler ordered and the chart alert written, so they land in `admission`
before any treatment decision; the `outcome` phase needs the MRI read in `seizure`, and the imaging item counts
only an order placed in `seizure` (`{"ordered": …, "phase": "seizure"}`), so no imaging is creditable in
hindsight. A head CT in `seizure` meets the critical item (CT first where MRI cannot be done promptly); the MRI
is still read to open the outcome.

Contraindicated acts are listed as safety events in every debrief. `plateletpheresis` is neutral (no acute
thrombosis or bleeding is recorded); `anagrelide` is taught through decision 4 only and is not offered as an
intervention, so the order list does not hint at the diagnosis.

**Safety outcome.** `aspirin` is `critical: true` (risk moderate): at platelet counts of 1905 and 1497 × 10⁹/L,
with an unexplained APTT of 52.3 s and no VWF result at any point, it is this case's dangerous error — possible
acquired von Willebrand syndrome turns it into a serious bleeding risk, and in a child under 16 aspirin needs a
specific indication — so giving it fails the assessment's safety outcome (`safety.passed: false`) whatever the
score. `heparin` is `critical: false` (risk high): anticoagulation without a demonstrated clot is a dangerous
misjudgement that the critical rubric item `rb-no-antithrombotic` already shows prominently, but it is recoverable
and not the error the case is built around. The other contraindicated items (antibiotics, levetiracetam, the
second cytoreductive drug) are low or moderate risk and recoverable.
