## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should prolonged APTT and thrombocytopenia be interpreted alongside thrombosis? Options: Consider acquired thrombophilia such as APS; prolonged APTT does not exclude thrombosis (supported: Imaging-confirmed clots and obstetric clues are present.) Long APTT means only bleeding, never thrombosis (distractor: Laboratory effects and in-vivo risk are not identical.) Clear lungs exclude pulmonary embolism (distractor: Normal auscultation does not negate vascular imaging.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: APS is strongly suspected but persistence and clinical details need verification (supported: One positive result is not complete classification evidence.) One aCL result satisfies every contemporary classification requirement (distractor: Isotype, persistence and complete scoring data are absent.) Negative ANA excludes primary APS (distractor: Primary APS does not require ANA positivity.)
- **management:** How should anticoagulation and follow-up be organised? Options: Specialists should individualise anticoagulation with platelet/bleeding review, repeat antibodies and pregnancy planning (supported: This report does not establish steroids or one anticoagulant for every APS patient.) Low platelets always prohibit anticoagulation (distractor: Actual thrombotic and bleeding risks require balancing.) Steroids alone replace thrombosis treatment (distractor: An immune label does not replace antithrombotic assessment.)
