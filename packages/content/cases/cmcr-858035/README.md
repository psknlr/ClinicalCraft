## Learning outcomes

1. Recognise an isolated prolonged APTT with a normal INR in a patient with venous thrombosis as a probable
   lupus-anticoagulant effect — a clotting, not a bleeding, tendency — and send lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin
   and anti-β2-glycoprotein I tests before the first heparin dose where this does not delay treatment (ISTH SSC 2020).
2. Start weight-based treatment-dose LMWH on the day of diagnosis for normotensive bilateral PE with DVT, a platelet
   count of 58 × 10⁹/L and occasional blood-streaked sputum, with daily platelet counts — no thrombolysis, no IVC
   filter and no delay to raise the platelets first (ESC 2019; AHA/ACC 2026; ISTH SSC 2018).
3. Explain what one anticardiolipin result of 104 U/mL (reference 0–12) without isotype, anti-β2GPI or lupus
   anticoagulant can and cannot establish under the 2023 ACR/EULAR APS classification criteria, and arrange repeat
   testing at least 12 weeks later while treating her as having APS.
4. Plan long-term anticoagulation for thrombotic APS: warfarin overlapping heparin for at least 5 days and until the
   INR is 2.0–3.0, then INR 2.0–3.0 with no planned stop, and no direct oral anticoagulant (ESC 2019; EULAR 2019;
   AHA/ACC 2026).
5. Counsel a woman of reproductive age who is starting warfarin: a pregnancy test first, no oestrogen-containing
   contraception (ACR 2020), and any pregnancy planned with the specialists, changing to treatment-dose LMWH plus
   low-dose aspirin once pregnancy is confirmed (EULAR 2019).
6. Compare the reported course — heparin then warfarin, with steroids given alongside and tapered — with current
   practice: no routine corticosteroids for a platelet count ≥ 30 × 10⁹/L without significant bleeding (ASH 2019,
   immune thrombocytopenia), and never steroids in place of anticoagulation.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

**Diagnosis (the report's): primary antiphospholipid syndrome presenting with bilateral pulmonary embolism and
proximal deep-vein thrombosis.** A 30-year-old woman had two weeks (半个月) of cough with a little white sputum,
occasionally streaked with blood, breathlessness on exertion relieved by rest and an occasional paroxysmal dull chest
pain with no clear relation to breathing, coughing or exertion — all worse for three days. Both legs ached mildly and
later developed pitting oedema. Background: "idiopathic thrombocytopenia" diagnosed several years earlier (details
unknown), a cholecystectomy, one stillbirth and no miscarriage (gestation not recorded), no smoking or alcohol.
Examination: no obvious cyanosis, clear breath sounds without crackles, mild bilateral pitting oedema, BP 130/80 mmHg.

CT: filling defects in both pulmonary arteries and in the right inferior (lower) pulmonary vein (1.0.0 notes that the
venous finding was not independently re-reviewed). Ultrasound: thrombosis of the left superficial femoral vein and
the right popliteal vein. Platelets 58 × 10⁹/L, APTT 71.6 s, INR normal, D-dimer 902 µg/L. Factors VIII, IX and XI
normal; ANA panel, ANCA and complement unremarkable; anticardiolipin 104 U/mL (reference 0–12) — the isotype, a repeat
at least 12 weeks later, lupus anticoagulant and anti-β2GPI are not recorded.

**Reported course:** heparin (low-molecular-weight heparin in the Chinese text of 1.0.0, "heparin" in the English),
then warfarin; steroids given at the same time and later tapered off. CT at 3 months: the clots had resolved;
platelets improved; no recurrence reported; the length of follow-up is unclear.

**Today's answer:** the long APTT with a normal INR in a woman who is clotting, with thrombocytopenia and a stillbirth,
points to a lupus anticoagulant and APS, not to a bleeding tendency. Send the antibody tests, then start treatment-dose
LMWH the same day with daily platelet counts (normotensive PE: no lysis; platelets ≥ 50 × 10⁹/L: full dose). Treat as
APS while confirming persistence at ≥ 12 weeks; long-term warfarin to an INR of 2.0–3.0 with no planned stop, no DOAC;
a pregnancy test before warfarin, no oestrogen-containing contraception, and pregnancy planned with the specialists.

### Critical items

- `rb-anticoagulated-day-one` — the missed life-saving act: bilateral PE with proximal DVT needs anticoagulation on the
  day it is found. The long APTT and the platelet count of 58 × 10⁹/L tempt learners to wait; the item is met only by an
  anticoagulant given in the admission phase once the platelet count is known, so anticoagulation after the antibody
  result counts as late. It never depends on an unrecorded finding.
- `rb-no-lysis` — the dangerous error avoided: systemic thrombolysis in a normotensive patient (130/80 mmHg) with
  thrombocytopenia and blood-streaked sputum (ESC 2019: no routine lysis in intermediate- or low-risk PE, class III).

**Safety outcome.** `systemic-lysis` is the case's only `critical: true` intervention (risk high, contraindicated in
every phase): alteplase for a normotensive PE with a low platelet count risks intracranial and pulmonary bleeding with
nothing to gain, so giving it fails the safety outcome whatever the score. The other harmful steps — IVC filter,
platelet transfusion, rivaroxaban, the combined pill (each `critical: false`, risk moderate), stacking LMWH and UFH, and
warfarin without heparin cover — are recoverable misjudgements that the next clinician can undo; they cost the
`rb-safe-choices` marks but do not fail the safety outcome.

## Embodied mode

at the bedside, the blood pressure included. The IV cannula (`iv-access`, nursing scope) comes from the
treatment cart; once it is in, laboratory tests can be ordered at a records terminal as well as at the bedside, and the
urine for the pregnancy test is collected at the bedside. The CT (`performedAt: provider`) needs her taken to the CT
scanner in imaging; the leg ultrasound, echo and chest X-ray come to the bed (`bedside`). The earlier haematology records,
the earlier obstetric records and the outcome are record reviews (`performedAt: records`, `requires: ["records"]`, risk
none), read at a records terminal or in the notes at the bedside. No intervention moves her; the phases have no
`location`. She speaks for herself throughout (a conscious adult; no companion, no `nonVerbalWhen`). Capabilities used —
`ct`, `ultrasound`, `echocardiography`, `xray`, `ecg-12lead`, `lab-hematology`, `lab-chemistry`, `lab-serology`,

## 中文摘要

**病例概要。** 30岁女性，咳嗽、少量白痰偶带血丝、活动后气短（休息可缓解）、偶有阵发性钝痛（与呼吸、咳嗽、劳累无明确关系）半个月，
近3天加重；双下肢先轻度胀痛、后出现可凹性水肿。既往数年前诊断“特发性血小板减少”（细节不详），胆囊切除，一次死胎、无流产
（孕周未记录），无烟酒嗜好。查体无明显发绀，双肺呼吸音清、无啰音，双下肢轻度凹陷性水肿，血压 130/80 mmHg。CT 示双肺动脉及右下肺
静脉充盈缺损（肺静脉所见未另行复核），超声示左股浅静脉和右腘静脉血栓；血小板 58×10⁹/L，APTT 71.6 s，INR 正常，D-二聚体
902 μg/L；凝血因子Ⅷ、Ⅸ、Ⅺ无异常；ANA谱、ANCA、补体等未见异常；抗心磷脂抗体 104 U/mL（参考 0–12），同型、12 周复查、狼疮抗凝物
和抗β2GPI 均未记录。报告诊断为原发性抗磷脂综合征：先用肝素（中文记录为低分子肝素）后改华法林，同时使用激素并逐渐停用；3 个月
CT 示血栓消失，血小板改善，无复发报告，随访时长不明。患者事实沿用 1.0.0 版的提取内容，尚未对照原文全文核对。

**学习目标。**

1. 把血栓患者单独延长的 APTT（INR 正常）识别为狼疮抗凝物样改变（易栓而非出血），首剂肝素前送检相关抗体但不推迟抗凝（ISTH SSC 2020）。
2. 血压正常、血小板 58×10⁹/L、偶有痰中带血的双侧肺栓塞，确诊当天开始治疗剂量低分子肝素并每天复查血小板；不溶栓、不放滤器、不先升血小板（ESC 2019；AHA/ACC 2026；ISTH SSC 2018）。
3. 按 2023 ACR/EULAR 分类标准解释一次抗心磷脂抗体结果能说明和不能说明的内容，至少 12 周后复查，同时按抗磷脂综合征治疗。
4. 长期抗凝：华法林与肝素重叠至少 5 天至 INR 2.0–3.0，之后维持 INR 2.0–3.0、不设停药期，不用直接口服抗凝药（ESC 2019；EULAR 2019；AHA/ACC 2026）。
5. 生育咨询：华法林前先查妊娠试验，不用含雌激素的避孕方法（ACR 2020），怀孕与专科计划，确认妊娠后改治疗剂量低分子肝素加小剂量阿司匹林（EULAR 2019）。
6. 对照报告与现行做法：血小板 ≥ 30×10⁹/L 且无明显出血时不常规用激素（ASH 2019），激素不能代替抗凝。

**关键决策。**

- 决策 1（入院当天）：APTT 延长而在长血栓——狼疮抗凝物样改变，怀疑抗磷脂综合征；干扰项为因子缺乏“自带抗凝”、弥散性血管内凝血、免疫性血小板减少症加偶然血栓。
- 决策 2（入院当天）：先抽好标本，当天开始治疗剂量低分子肝素、每天复查血小板、不溶栓；干扰项为按 APTT 调整普通肝素、先用激素升血小板、全身溶栓、放滤器不抗凝。
- 决策 3（住院期间）：按抗磷脂综合征处理，但一次结果证明不了持续阳性，12 周后复查同型、抗β2GPI 和狼疮抗凝物；干扰项为已可确诊、ANA 阴性即排除、停抗凝两周再查。
- 决策 4：华法林与肝素重叠，INR 2.0–3.0 长期维持，不用直接口服抗凝药；干扰项为利伐沙班、3 个月停药、INR 3–4 加阿司匹林。
- 决策 5：先查妊娠试验、不用雌激素避孕、与专科计划怀孕、确认妊娠后改低分子肝素加阿司匹林；干扰项为复方避孕药并停华法林备孕、孕期继续华法林、只用阿司匹林。

**安全结果。** 只有 `systemic-lysis`（全身溶栓）标为 `critical: true`：对血压正常、血小板减少的患者溶栓有颅内及肺出血风险而无获益，给予即判安全结果不通过；滤器、输血小板、利伐沙班、复方避孕药、两种肝素叠加和无肝素覆盖的华法林为可纠正的失误（`critical: false`），只扣 `rb-safe-choices` 的分。

**复盘要点。**

- 狼疮抗凝物：试管里凝血慢，体内易栓；两种方法检测，最好抗凝前采血，但不为采血推迟治疗（ISTH SSC 2020）。
- 确诊当天抗凝：低分子肝素优于普通肝素，中低危肺栓塞不溶栓，滤器只用于抗凝绝对禁忌；血小板 ≥ 50×10⁹/L 可用全量（ESC 2019；AHA/ACC 2026；ISTH SSC 2018）。
