## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which cross-organ working interpretation is most reasonable? Options: Assess disseminated tuberculosis and urinary involvement alongside renal deterioration and obstetric risk (supported: Miliary opacities and persistent urinary abnormalities need a unifying assessment.) A simple upper respiratory infection explains everything (distractor: It does not explain renal deterioration, haematuria and miliary opacities.) Physiological gestational hydronephrosis explains all urine abnormalities (distractor: Marked haematuria, pyuria and creatinine elevation need pathological assessment.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Pulmonary disease has molecular support; renal evidence is more indirect (supported: Positive pulmonary testing is not renal histological confirmation.) Negative smears exclude tuberculosis (distractor: Negative smears do not negate positive molecular and clinical findings.) All renal injury is proven to be caused by tuberculosis (distractor: Obstruction, infection and other contributors are not fully distinguished.)
- **management:** How should maternal, fetal and renal risks be coordinated? Options: Obstetric, TB and renal teams should individualise treatment and delivery plans, including renal effects on medicines (supported: The reported induction is case-specific and does not mean every pregnancy with TB requires termination.) All pregnancies with TB must be terminated immediately (distractor: A single report cannot establish a universal obstetric indication.) Active infection need not be addressed before delivery (distractor: Maternal disease and organ risk require timely specialist attention.)
