## Learning outcomes

By the end of the case the learner can:

1. Recognise drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS) when fever, a rapidly spreading rash
   with facial oedema and liver injury begin 2–6 weeks after a drug was started (NICE CG183, 2014), work out the
   absolute eosinophil count from the differential, and score the RegiSCAR items the record allows (Kardaun 2007).
2. Rank the suspect drugs by timing — the drug started four to five weeks before the illness, not the antibiotic
   added after the rash — and examine the mucosae and any peeling to exclude an SJS/TEN overlap (BAD 2016).
3. Treat DRESS with severe liver involvement: keep the culprit stopped, add no NSAID or non-essential new drug, and
   start a systemic corticosteroid (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day in severe DRESS; French national DRESS protocol, PNDS
   2024) tapered over 6 weeks to 3 months or longer (international Delphi consensus 2024).
4. Recognise threatened liver failure when the rash improves but bilirubin rises and prothrombin activity falls,
   monitor conscious level, asterixis and glucose, and discuss the patient with a liver transplant centre
   (ACG 2021; EASL 2019).
5. Manage fever and jaundice returning weeks into the illness by looking for infection, viral reactivation and
   newly added drugs before restoring the corticosteroid dose and tapering more slowly (Delphi consensus 2024).
6. Prevent re-exposure: record the culprit as a severe delayed reaction with an alert card, avoid NSAIDs until an
   allergist has advised, never rechallenge, refer to a drug-allergy service, and follow up for late autoimmune
   complications (NICE CG183, 2014; Kano 2015).

**Learning outcomes (6) · may reveal the diagnosis** disclosure and list them in every debrief (CASE_AUTHORING §9.4,
GLOSSARY §4). The six HUD `objectives` below are the in-game task list.

## Clinical summary (spoilers)

A 29-year-old woman had taken sustained-release ibuprofen continuously for knee pain from 39 days before admission
(dose not recorded). A year earlier an ibuprofen-containing product had given her a widespread maculopapular rash
that cleared when it was stopped. Seven days before admission she developed fever (highest 41 °C) with a marked sore
throat; the next day erythema appeared on the face and trunk, spread rapidly over the whole body and became
confluent, with itch and swollen eyelids; jaundice with yellow urine followed (4 days before admission). Penicillin
was given for two days after the fever and rash had begun; both drugs were then stopped, and after four more days of
treatment (not recorded) she still had a high fever, with the skin lesions and the jaundice worse.

On admission: temperature 39.2 °C, BP 96/60 (no unit in 1.0.0, so none is shown); oedematous facial erythema with
scaling, peeling of the trunk, a confluent maculopapular rash on the limbs, oral ulcers and blood-crusted lips,
scleral icterus, enlarged tender submandibular nodes and mild lower-limb oedema. White cells 11.62×10⁹/L with an
eosinophil fraction of 0.067 (6.7%), haemoglobin 92 g/L; total/direct bilirubin 133.6/117.5 μmol/L, ALT 585 U/L,
AST 878 U/L, albumin 33.9 g/L, prothrombin activity 64%. The hepatitis viruses tested, EBV IgM and CMV IgM were
negative; the chest X-ray and abdominal ultrasound were essentially normal at first; no HHV-6 result is recorded.

Five days later the fever had subsided and the rash lessened, but bilirubin had risen to 255.9 μmol/L, albumin fallen
to 26.3 g/L and prothrombin activity to 37%, with new ascites; no encephalopathy assessment or INR is recorded. The
report calls this liver failure. With glucocorticoids and several supportive medicines (version 1.0.0 describes them
as including a combination of liver-protective drugs) she improved; fever and jaundice returned on day 25, recovery
was then gradual, liver tests were normal by about day 38, and there was no recurrence in the year after the
medicines were stopped. Drug names, doses, start dates and the taper are not recorded.

**Diagnosis (as taught):** drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS; the report's term is drug-
induced hypersensitivity syndrome, 药物超敏反应综合征) with ibuprofen the probable culprit, severe liver involvement
progressing to what the report calls liver failure, and a relapse on day 25. The oral ulcers, blood-crusted
lips and truncal peeling require an SJS/TEN overlap to be excluded; whether it was is not recorded. RegiSCAR, as far
as the record allows: about 3–5 on admission (possible to probable) and about 4–6 once the course outlasts 15 days
(probable to definite) — see the numbers check.

Ordering cannot be scored by the condition language (CMCR_CASES "Known limits"), so the time-critical acts are gated
(see the timeline). The do-nothing path earns 0 of 28 because `rb-avoided-harm` counts only once a decision where the
harm was possible has been attempted or a harmful act given. The six harmful acts are contraindicated in every phase;
the two re-exposures cost the critical item, the other four `rb-avoided-harm`.

**Safety outcome.** `oral-ibuprofen` is `critical: true`: giving the culprit again — for fever, for pain or as a
"challenge" — during or after DRESS with jaundice risks a faster, more severe and potentially fatal relapse (NICE
CG183 2014; ACG 2021 recommends strongly against re-exposure after drug-induced liver injury with jaundice), so it fails
the assessment's safety outcome (`safety.passed: false`) whatever the score, and every debrief and export shows it
beside the score. It is the only high-risk contraindicated intervention. The other contraindicated orders are
moderate- or low-risk misjudgements that another clinician can still stop or reverse — another NSAID such as naproxen
(`nsaid-analgesic`), an empirical β-lactam, an abrupt steroid stop, a liver-protective combination, an early
discharge — so they leave the safety outcome alone and cost marks instead: the NSAID the critical rubric item
`rb-no-reexposure`, the rest `rb-avoided-harm`.

2. **Description** — "Take me through her first day: what did you think it was, what did you stop, what did you

   * "Put the drugs and the symptoms on one line. Why is the penicillin not the culprit?"

   * "Which RegiSCAR items could you score on the first day, and which only weeks later?"
   * "What in her mouth, on her lips and on her trunk made you think of SJS/TEN, and how would you settle it?"
   * "Fever 39.2 °C, BP 96/60, white cells 11.62 — when would you give antibiotics, and which would you avoid?"
   * "Her liver involvement decided the steroid dose. What made it severe?"
   * "On day 5 the skin was better. What told you the liver was not — and what was missing to call it liver failure?"
   * "What would you have said on the phone to the transplant centre?"
   * "She relapsed on day 25. What were you looking for before you increased the steroid?"
   * "She had reacted to an ibuprofen product a year earlier. How do you think she came to take ibuprofen again, and

   * "The report's team used several liver-protective drugs together. What would you say to them today?"
4. **Summary** — "What will you do the next time a patient has a fever and a spreading rash weeks after starting a

下肢轻度水肿。白细胞11.62×10⁹/L，嗜酸性粒细胞比例0.067（绝对值约0.78×10⁹/L），血红蛋白92 g/L；总胆红素133.6、直接
胆红素117.5 μmol/L，ALT 585、AST 878 U/L，白蛋白33.9 g/L，凝血酶原活动度64%；所查肝炎病毒、EB病毒和巨细胞病毒IgM阴性，
胸片和腹部超声起初基本正常，人类疱疹病毒6型没有结果记录。五天后热退疹减，总胆红素却升至255.9 μmol/L，白蛋白26.3 g/L，凝血酶原

原文全文核对。

**学习目标。**
1. 在开始用药后2–6周出现发热、迅速扩展的皮疹伴面部水肿和肝损伤时识别DRESS（NICE CG183，2014），算出嗜酸性粒细胞绝对值，
   按已有资料计算RegiSCAR评分（Kardaun 2007）。
2. 按时间顺序判断可疑致敏药——起病前4–5周开始的布洛芬，而不是出疹后加用的青霉素——并排查SJS/TEN重叠（英国皮肤科医师协会2016）。
3. 肝脏严重受累的DRESS：保持致敏药停用，不用非甾体抗炎药和非必需新药，全身糖皮质激素（重症DRESS泼尼松1 mg/kg/d，法国PNDS 2024），
   6周至3个月内缓慢减量（2024年国际Delphi共识）。
4. 皮疹好转而胆红素上升、凝血酶原活动度下降时识别肝衰竭风险，监测意识状态和血糖，联系肝移植中心（ACG 2021；EASL 2019）。
5. 数周后再次发热、黄疸时，先排查感染、病毒再激活和新加药物，再恢复激素剂量并放慢减量。
6. 防止再接触：登记严重迟发型反应并发警示卡，专科评估前避免非甾体抗炎药，绝不再激发，转诊药物过敏专科，随访迟发性自身免疫并发症
   （NICE CG183，2014；Kano 2015）。

**关键决策。**
1. 工作诊断：布洛芬引起的DRESS，肝脏受累严重，需排查SJS/TEN重叠（干扰项：青霉素所致SJS/TEN、成人斯蒂尔病、中毒性休克）。
2. 第一天：两药继续停用、不加非甾体抗炎药和非必需新药、留血培养、泼尼松1 mg/kg/d加皮肤护理、每天查肝功能和凝血
   （干扰项：单用外用激素、按脓毒症先上头孢、激素冲击3天后骤停）。
3. 五天后：按肝衰竭风险处理——查意识状态、扑翼样震颤、血糖和INR，当天联系肝移植中心，激素不骤停（干扰项：停激素改多种保肝药
   〔接近报告当年的支持治疗〕、减激素出院、先处理腹水）。
4. 第25天：先按DRESS复发处理——核对新加药物、血培养、HHV-6和CMV DNA、肝功能，激素恢复起始剂量后更慢地减量。
5. 出院：登记布洛芬并发警示卡，专科评估前避免所有非甾体抗炎药（含复方制剂），注明青霉素为出疹后加用，转诊过敏专科，随访甲功、
   血糖和肝功能（干扰项：布洛芬激发试验、改用萘普生、青霉素类终身标为过敏且不转诊）。

**安全结果。** `oral-ibuprofen` 标为 `critical: true`：DRESS伴黄疸之后再次给予布洛芬（无论退热、止痛还是“激发”）可能引起更快、更重甚至致命的复发，给予即判安全结果不通过（`safety.passed: false`），无论得分多少，所有复盘和导出都在分数旁显示；其他禁忌医嘱（萘普生等其他非甾体抗炎药、经验性β-内酰胺类、骤停激素、多种保肝药联用、提前出院）属可纠正的判断失误，不影响安全结果，但要扣分：其他非甾体抗炎药使关键评分项 `rb-no-reexposure` 不达标，其余各项扣除 `rb-avoided-harm` 的分数。

**复盘要点。** 用药时间线决定嫌疑药物；嗜酸性粒细胞要看绝对值；口腔、唇部和躯干表现要求排查SJS/TEN；治疗强度跟着肝脏走；
皮肤好转不等于肝脏好转，胆红素上升、凝血酶原活动度下降时要找肝性脑病、查INR、联系肝移植中心；第三周以后的复发先找诱因；
终身记录并避免致敏药，过敏专科评估代替再激发。两项关键行为：登记过敏且不再接触非甾体抗炎药，以及在复发阶段之前完成肝衰竭
监测和肝移植中心联系。尚待核实的细节见上文“To verify”。本稿尚未经执业医师审核。
