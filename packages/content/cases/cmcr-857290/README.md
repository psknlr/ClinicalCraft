## Clinical summary

Historical imaging and course support chronic thromboembolic pulmonary vascular disease, with gaps in modern confirmation. Minor parenchymal changes do not explain the whole picture. Right-heart thickening and adherent clot support chronicity. Associated disease is not sole causation.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Which mechanism deserves priority from the early evidence? Options: Pulmonary vascular obstruction and right-heart load rather than left-heart failure or minor pneumonia alone (supported: Hypoxaemia, right-heart changes and DVT form a coherent pattern.) Normal LVEF excludes all cardiopulmonary disease (distractor: Right-heart and pulmonary circulation abnormalities remain.) Minor CT opacities fully explain severe hypoxaemia (distractor: The imaging burden does not match clinical severity.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Eccentric adherent thrombus and right-ventricular thickening support chronicity, while modern CTEPH confirmation remains incomplete (supported: Echo pressure estimation is not catheterisation and haemodynamics cannot be invented.) Vasculitis is proven as the sole thrombotic cause (distractor: The source proposes an association without proving sole causation.) All thrombi must have arisen within eight days (distractor: A year of symptoms and right-wall thickening suggest a longer process.)
- **management:** How should a current plan avoid the historical limitation? Options: Refer to a PH/CTEPH centre for confirmation and multidisciplinary surgical, interventional, medical and anticoagulation assessment (supported: Age or bilateral disease alone cannot replace modern operability assessment.) Permanently declare inoperability from age 69 and bilateral thrombi (distractor: An experienced team must assess accessible lesions and comorbidity.) Improvement with NIV removes need for thrombotic care (distractor: Supportive improvement does not mean vascular obstruction has resolved.)
