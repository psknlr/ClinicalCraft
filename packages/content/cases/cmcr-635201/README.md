## Clinical summary

The report describes clinical Bardet–Biedl syndrome with obesity, retinal disease and diabetes without molecular confirmation. Congenital digits and progressive visual loss identify a syndromic pattern. Clinical diagnosis differs from a specific gene diagnosis. Hypoglycaemia and accessible communication are practical safety issues.

## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** Can this be treated as ordinary obesity-associated diabetes alone? Options: Assess syndromic obesity by integrating congenital digits, retinal and developmental findings (supported: The multisystem pattern cannot be attributed to diet alone.) Attribute everything to poor self-discipline (distractor: This ignores congenital and progressive organ findings and is inappropriate communication.) Two weeks of detected hyperglycaemia explains visual loss since nine (distractor: The chronology does not support that attribution.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: The clinical BBS phenotype is substantial, while genetics and long-term organ risks still need assessment (supported: One normal creatinine does not establish absence of future renal risk.) Consanguinity proves a particular BBS gene (distractor: An inheritance clue does not identify a gene.) Every BBS patient must already have hypogonadism (distractor: Phenotypes vary and this case had regular menses and documented normal external genitalia.)
- **management:** What should a current long-term plan emphasise? Options: Coordinate genetics, eye, renal and metabolic care with accessible communication, hypoglycaemia prevention and review of current options (supported: Approved treatment for some BBS-related obesity now exists; the historical absence of specific therapy is not a permanent conclusion.) Stop reviewing treatment because the old article said none existed (distractor: Evidence and authorisations change over time.) Copy historical polypharmacy without considering hypoglycaemia or communication limits (distractor: Frequent hypoglycaemia occurred later and requires prevention and adjustment.)
