## Decisions and assessment

- **diagnosis:** How should prenatal exposure enter the seizure differential? Options: Include drug-related neonatal adaptation/withdrawal or extrapyramidal effects while continuing seizure assessment (supported: Exposure and early symptoms support consideration without bypassing infectious, metabolic or neurological causes.) Prenatal medication permits stopping all investigation (distractor: Association alone is not causal confirmation.) Apgar 10 directly establishes severe birth asphyxia (distractor: The source does not supply that evidence.)
- **evidence:** Which conclusion is supported by the subsequent records? Options: Results reduce support for some common causes without proving one drug mechanism (supported: Without drug levels or EEG, withdrawal and residual drug effects cannot be precisely distinguished.) Normal two-month follow-up proves both medicines completely safe in pregnancy (distractor: One short-term outcome cannot establish general safety.) Clozapine alone is proven responsible (distractor: Combined exposure and missing tests prevent sole attribution.)
- **management:** What should current care and family communication include? Options: Monitor neonatal breathing, feeding and seizures with neonatal/perinatal psychiatric review; avoid unsupervised maternal abrupt withdrawal (supported: Current labels support monitoring exposed neonates while maternal treatment remains individualised.) Tell the mother to stop all psychiatric medication on her own (distractor: This risks maternal relapse and requires professional benefit–risk assessment.) Automatically sedate every exposed neonate using the old regimen (distractor: Treatment depends on actual symptoms and assessment.)
