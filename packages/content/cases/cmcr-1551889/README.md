## Clinical summary (spoilers)

Double aortic arch was detected late in pregnancy; right dominance was confirmed after birth and left-arch division was followed by favourable six-month symptoms.

- Review routine fetal ultrasound: Growth, fluid and placental location were reported normal; cardiac situs, four-chamber appearance and outflow tracts appeared normal.
- Review the three-vessel-and-trachea view: A right aortic arch and left ductus with another arch-like structure left of the trachea formed a complete ring and “9” configuration; each subclavian artery arose from its respective arch.
- Review airway and Doppler records: The trachea crossed the ring with an indentation; neither arch showed flow obstruction. Fetal movement and small structures prevented reliable prenatal identification of the dominant arch.
- Review genetic counselling: Amniocentesis was offered for potentially associated chromosomal abnormalities including 22q11.2 deletion; the couple declined, so no confirmation is available.
