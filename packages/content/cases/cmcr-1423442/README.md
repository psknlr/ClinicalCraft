## Learning outcomes

After the case the learner can:

1. Identify an acquired proximal tubulopathy from paired blood and urine
   results (glycosuria with a normal blood glucose, α1-microglobulinuria,
   hypouricaemia, renal potassium loss, hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis),
   and judge a urine phosphate of 13 mmol/24 h as renal phosphate wasting
   when the serum phosphate is 0.53 mmol/L.
2. Explain bone pain, multiple rib fractures, height loss, a raised ALP and
   persistent hypophosphataemia as osteomalacia that can coexist with
   osteoporosis, and explain why calcium alone failed.
3. Attribute the tubulopathy to adefovir with a structured causality
   assessment (drug timeline, dechallenge, alternatives) and use the recorded
   tests to exclude myeloma or light-chain disease, primary
   hyperparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency alone and distal RTA.
4. Switch antiviral therapy safely according to the Chinese 2022 and EASL
   hepatitis B guidance — stop adefovir and start entecavir or tenofovir
   alafenamide the same day, never TDF and never a gap — and plan phosphate,
   active vitamin D with calcium, potassium and alkali, with monitoring.
5. Explain why a "normal" creatinine hid kidney damage in a thin, bed-bound
   man, and list what long-term nucleotide-analogue users need monitored
   beyond creatinine (the authors' list).

These are the case's `learningOutcomes`; the six HUD `objectives` below are
the in-game task list.

## Clinical summary

**Presentation (day 0).** A 46-year-old man is admitted to the endocrine ward
with 3 years of chest and back pain and 1 year of progressive limb weakness.
The pain began with activity-related chest and back pain and lumbar ache,
eased a little by painkillers and plasters; chest X-rays at several hospitals
were unremarkable. It then concentrated at the costal margins and scapulae and
stopped responding to analgesia. About 11 months before admission his legs
weakened (left, then right) from difficulty with stairs to difficulty on the
flat, with lightning pains in the back and legs and difficulty turning,
sitting up and standing. He has lost 3 kg and 3 cm of height. No long-term
steroids, no prior bed rest. Chronic hepatitis B for more than ten years,
treated with **adefovir dipivoxil 10 mg daily for nearly ten years**.

**The missed clue (about 5 months before admission, orthopaedics).** EMG
unremarkable; thoracolumbar MRI with degenerative change and abnormal signal in
some vertebrae; bone densitometry "osteoporosis"; bone scan with multiple
active foci; chest X-ray with multiple bilateral rib fractures. Liver and renal
function, calcium and PTH normal — and **serum phosphate 0.45–0.65 mmol/L
(0.80–1.65)**. He was given oral calcium only, without benefit.

**Examination.** T 36.5 °C, P 80/min, **RR 25/min**, BP 120/80 mmHg. Thin;
unable to walk. Tenderness over the cervical and upper thoracic vertebrae,
scapulae and ribs; costal margins everted and locally thickened. Power grade
4; straight-leg raise positive. The rest is summarised as unremarkable.

**Admission results.**

| test | result (source ranges) |
|---|---|
| blood | K 3.1 mmol/L, Cl 111 mmol/L, Ca 2.2 mmol/L, **P 0.53 mmol/L**, creatinine 103 μmol/L, **urate 117 μmol/L** (160–430), fasting glucose 4.1 mmol/L, **ALP 201 U/L** (32–121) |
| urinalysis | **glucose ++**, protein + |
| 24-hour urine | microalbumin 70.9 mg/L (0–19.1), **α1-microglobulin 168.0 mg/L** (0–12.5), protein 0.93 g/24 h (0–0.14), **phosphate 13 mmol/24 h** (16–48), potassium 79 mmol/24 h (25–100) |
| blood gas | pH 7.24, PaCO₂ 31 mmHg, HCO₃⁻ 13 mmol/L |
| 25-hydroxyvitamin D₃ | 36.97, unit printed "mmol/L" (read as nmol/L; low) |
| hepatitis B | HBsAg, anti-HBe, anti-HBc positive; HBV DNA < 4.2 × 10² copies/mL ((0–5) × 10²) |
| marrow | essentially normal; haematological tumour markers normal |
| renal scintigraphy | moderately impaired glomerular filtration in both kidneys; GFR below normal (no figure) |

**Diagnosis.** Adefovir-induced acquired Fanconi syndrome with
hypophosphataemic osteomalacia (coexisting with osteoporosis).

**Reported course.** Day 9: adefovir stopped; entecavir 0.5 mg daily,
calcium carbonate–D₃ 0.6 g daily, calcitriol 1 μg daily, oral potassium
chloride, plus diammonium glycyrrhizinate 100 mg tds, reduced glutathione
1.8 g IV daily and Huganning tablets 4 tds. Day 11: phosphate 0.57 mmol/L →
phosphate supplements added. Day 14: 0.71 mmol/L. Day 18: pain much better,
still weak; urine phosphate 16 and potassium 37 mmol/24 h, protein 0.31 g/24 h;
discharged on entecavir with phosphate and calcium. About 6 months after
discharge: phosphate 0.81 mmol/L, K 3.6 mmol/L, urate 153 μmol/L, urine protein
±, glucose negative; walking with long strides, much less pain.

  examination are `admission`-only. `course` opens when objectives 1–5 are done

Hints coach with questions ("When the body is short of phosphate, what does a
healthy kidney do with it?") and never contain an option's text.

### Decisions

- `phosphate-wasting` (single; opens when the admission blood and 24-hour
  urine are read): 13 mmol/24 h with serum phosphate 0.53 mmol/L is
  inappropriately high (≈ 400 mg/day). Distractors: "low — the kidney is
  conserving" (the reading the 1.0.0 case taught), "uninterpretable without
  FEPO₄", "a shift into cells".
- `diagnosis` (single; opens after the medication history and the blood,
  urinalysis, 24-hour urine, blood gas, vitamin D, marrow and earlier
  orthopaedic bloods): adefovir-induced Fanconi syndrome with osteomalacia.
  Distractors: myeloma/light-chain tubulopathy (marrow normal), vitamin D
  deficiency alone, distal RTA, primary osteoporosis with "normal creatinine"
  (the three-year misdiagnosis).
- `antiviral` (single; after `diagnosis` and the HBV results): stop adefovir
  and switch the same day to entecavir (as reported) or TAF. Distractors: TDF
  (same tubular toxicity), stop all antivirals (flare), continue adefovir at a
  longer interval plus more calcium (the authors' "adjust the dose" option).
- `metabolic` (select all; after `diagnosis`, blood gas and vitamin D):
  phosphate now, calcitriol with calcium, potassium with alkali. Distractors:
  a bisphosphonate now; "the tubule recovers quickly — calcium only".

### How wrong choices score

- Decisions are scored with `decidedFirstTry`: guessing through the options
  costs the item.
- `tdf`, `stop-all-antivirals`, `adefovir-reduced` and `bisphosphonate` are
  always `contraindicated` (safety events with a `harm` text) and fail
  `r-safety-hbv` or `r-safety-bone`.
- The report's low-value co-medications (`glycyrrhizinate`, `glutathione-iv`,
  `huganning`) are neutral: their responses explain that no indication is
  recorded (and that glycyrrhizin preparations can worsen hypokalaemia). They
  are not scored, because they were the report's practice.
- `r-safety-bone` is a harm-avoidance item. Until the framework's `scoredWhen`
  lands it is earned by doing nothing; the annotation file gives it
  `scoredWhen` (see Embodied mode).

### Debrief questions (PEARLS)

   ago that nobody had acted on?"
2. **Description.** "Summarise the case in two sentences: what was wrong and
   what did you change?"
3. **Analysis.**
   - "Why is 13 mmol of phosphate a day too much in this man, when the lab
     flags it as low?"
   - "Which results put the problem in the proximal tubule rather than the
     bone or the distal tubule?"
   - "What would you need to see before calling this myeloma? What was
     missing from the record?"
   - "Why not TDF? Why not simply stop the antivirals?"
   - "Why did calcium alone fail, and why not a bisphosphonate?"
   - "How did the 'normal' creatinine mislead, and what would you monitor in
     anyone on adefovir or TDF?"
4. **Summary.** "What will you check next time a patient on a nucleotide
   analogue has bone pain or a low phosphate?"

### Teaching notes

- The report's same-day switch to entecavir matches today's guidance; its late
  phosphate (day 11), the absence of an alkali and the hepatoprotective
  co-medications are the points where practice differs.
- The discharge urine phosphate (16 mmol/24 h) was taken on phosphate
  supplements and largely reflects the dose; the report calls it improved,
  but it cannot show tubular recovery on its own (result note).

## Embodied mode

- **Grounding.** Laboratory tests are sampled at the patient (ward bed); the
  24-hour urine requires urinalysis and chemistry and is ordered at the
  bedside. The marrow requires `cytology` (ordered at the pathology or
  laboratory station). The five chart reviews (`prior-labs`, `bone`,
  `renal-scintigraphy`, `inpatient-course`, `followup`) require only
  `records`. The facility has no nuclear-medicine camera, so the renal
  scintigraphy is read as the report filed in the chart.
- **Interventions.** Drugs need `medications` (delivered); the only IV item
  (reduced glutathione, low value) needs `iv-access`, so the case has an
  `iv-access` procedure; the reference path gives no IV drug. Stopping a drug
  and the ADR report are kind `other`, scope `medical`.
- **Where the patient goes.** Nowhere: he stays in his ward bed; no study in
  the reference path needs a fixed device. Posture `lying`; build `slim`.
- **Framework annotations** (handed to the coordinator, applied once the
  fields land in the schema): `performedAt: "records"`
  for the five chart reviews; `r-safety-bone.scoredWhen` (attempted
  `metabolic` or any bone/mineral prescription); `critical: true` on
  `r-safety-hbv` and `r-inv-records`.
- Offline autopilot (2026-10-28): clinical and embodied, zh-CN and en, all
  23/23 (100 %), every objective done.

## 中文摘要

**病例概要。** 46岁男性，胸背部疼痛3年、进行性四肢乏力1年，入院时不能行走。慢性乙型肝炎10余年，服用阿德福韦酯10 mg/日近10年。入院前约5个月骨科检查已发现双侧多发肋骨骨折、骨质疏松，血磷0.45–0.65 mmol/L，却只予补钙，无效。入院查体：呼吸25次/分，消瘦，脊柱、肩胛骨和肋骨压痛，肋弓外翻、增粗，肌力4级。化验：血磷0.53 mmol/L、血钾3.1 mmol/L、血氯111 mmol/L、尿酸117 μmol/L、ALP 201 U/L，空腹血糖4.1 mmol/L而尿糖++，α1微球蛋白168 mg/L，尿磷13 mmol/24 h，尿钾79 mmol/24 h，pH 7.24、HCO₃⁻ 13 mmol/L；骨髓象正常。诊断：阿德福韦酯致范科尼综合征合并低磷性骨软化。原报告第9天停药换用恩替卡韦，给予钙剂、骨化三醇、氯化钾，第11天加用补磷；出院后约6个月血磷0.81 mmol/L，能大步行走。

**学习目标。**

1. 根据配对的血、尿结果识别获得性近端肾小管病，并在血磷0.53 mmol/L时把尿磷13 mmol/24 h判定为肾性失磷。
2. 把骨痛、骨折、身高下降、ALP升高和持续低磷解释为可与骨质疏松并存的骨软化，说明单纯补钙为何无效。
3. 用结构化因果评估归因于阿德福韦酯，并用已有检查排除骨髓瘤、甲旁亢、单纯维生素D缺乏和远端肾小管酸中毒。
4. 依据中国2022年版和EASL乙肝指南安全换药（恩替卡韦或TAF，不用TDF，不中断），并规划补磷、活性维生素D加钙、补钾和纠酸及监测。
5. 说明“肌酐正常”为何掩盖肾损害，列出核苷酸类似物使用者应监测的指标。

**关键决策。**

- 尿磷解读：血磷很低时每天约400 mg尿磷是不适当的肾性失磷，不能用实验室参考值判为“偏低”。
- 统一诊断：阿德福韦酯致范科尼综合征伴骨软化；骨髓瘤、单纯维生素D缺乏、远端RTA和原发性骨质疏松都不能解释全部证据。
- 换药：当天换用恩替卡韦（原报告做法）或TAF；TDF有同样的肾小管毒性；停用全部抗病毒药会导致肝炎加重。
- 代谢治疗（多选）：现在就补磷，骨化三醇加钙，补钾并用碱剂纠正酸中毒；不用双膦酸盐，不等肾小管“自行恢复”。

**复盘要点。**

- 尿液结果要和同一时间的血液结果配对解读；低磷时肾脏本应几乎不排磷。
- 骨质疏松和骨软化可以并存；补钙无效本身就是线索。
- 换药不停药；不用TDF；上报药品不良反应。
- 原报告在第11天才加用补磷、没有记录碱剂，并用了缺乏记录指征的保肝药——这些是与今天做法不同之处。
- 消瘦、卧床者的肌酐会掩盖肾损害；长期用阿德福韦酯者要监测血磷、尿蛋白、尿糖和GFR等。
